Why did the PTS or
Goldilocks fail?

@@b1647 p
Figure: The point and figure chart with volume per column, marking
points 1 to 3 and asking why the PTS failed. Real data: EURUSD point and
figure from 5-minute closes, box 0.0004 (4 pips), 3-box reversal, 24 Feb
– 1 Mar 2022. (The original chart was a USDCAD point and figure chart,
August 2021; this one shows the same structure. The volume readings in
the text describe the original chart.)

@@b1648 ul
<li><p><strong>At 1:</strong> there is a large amount of sales. Buyers
agree and take it, so new buyers absorb all the offers.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>At 2:</strong> the market is in the Return Zone. The
amount of sales shows many new buyers entering here. But can those
buyers support the market? Is there any pulling force?</p></li>
<li><p><strong>At 3:</strong> the market moves up with no change of
property. There is no amount of sales in the volume, so no new buyers
can support the market.</p></li>

@@b1649 p
For the market to move up, it needs new buyers, or at least no
sellers who can push it down. So the market moved down to test its
Matrix Overlay, the FMX.

@@b1650 p
This shows clearly why the market always follows the Nash
equilibrium. Participants always look for maximum profit. At 3 the
buyers sat at a very low Nash point; no one wanted such a move. Buyers
want a lot of profit, so the market moved down to test.
