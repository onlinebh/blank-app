@@b7 h1
Foreword: Six years of
letters

@@b8 p
In September 2020 I wrote to Michael Archer to thank him for The
Goodman CODEX. I had searched for it everywhere, and I told him it had
saved my world. He answered. Six years and thousands of messages later,
I know it was the best reply I ever received.

@@b9 p
I never met Charles B. Goodman. Michael did, for eleven years, from a
July morning in 1973 at the Peavey office in Denver. Everything I know
of the man, his laugh, his silences, the way he called Michael "Dad", I
know because Michael wrote it down for me and kept writing, one email at
a time.

@@b10 p
This book gathers those emails. It also gathers my side of the
conversation: my questions, my doubts, the books I read, the arguments
we had and the research I sent back. A teacher gives, and a student
answers. Both belong here.

@@b11 p
<strong>How to read it.</strong> Every chapter is short and has one
idea. Each opens with a line that names the idea, tells the story in
Michael's words, says what Charlie meant, and ends with something you
can try on your next chart. Read one chapter a day or read the whole
book on a flight. The order is built so that each chapter prepares the
next.

@@b12 p
Michael once wrote to me, "If it wasn't for you all the stories of
Charlie would surely be lost." It is the kindest thing anyone has said
to me. It is also not true: it was Michael's memory that kept them alive
for fifty years. I only held the page.

@@b19 h2
My Trading Explained (2023)

@@b20 p
<em>I wrote this walk-through for Michael in 2023: two real trades,
on GBPCHF and NZDJPY, chart by chart, as I saw them. For this book every
chart has been redrawn from real market data with the same structure;
each caption names the data used.</em>

@@b21 p
In these few pages I explain how I trade every day, long or short.
First of all, I trade with defined risk, suited to my equity. My
psychology must be ready: a quiet place and a mind clear of everything
else, so I can be proactive with the market. I listen to music until the
wanted moment jumps in front of me. It usually takes ten to thirty
minutes to sync with the market; matcha or chamomile tea helps.

@@b22 p
These are the pairs I use for short-term trading. Every week I plan
on the daily chart, then zoom in to find a low-risk trade. A trade that
does not fit the daily chart, I filter out: most of the time it is
unable to propagate.

@@b33 p
On the 2-minute chart I look for the Dagger, the spark where buyers
must start their propagation.

@@b34 p
This is how a bad trade looks: buyers and sellers are not in
consensus. The 1-hour is still selling; the 5-minute shows temporary
buying from profit-taking; the 2-minute is unable to break the upper
line of the Dagger, the line where sellers renew their selling to
protect the downtrend. Trades like this can break an inexperienced
trader's account. Goodman theory is based on PROPAGATION!

@@b49 p
Stop loss on the 2-minute chart. Buy, and watch how buyers propagate
on the 2-minute. There are different ways to buy; some buy at the break
of the trendline, but with the spread on a 2-minute chart there is not
much difference. Just buy anywhere near the bottom, and watch how the
buying comes in.

@@b51 p
Figure: The nested 6&amp;6: the upper and lower lines close in after

chart was NZDJPY, 2-minute, 3–7 March 2023; this one shows the same
structure.)

@@b52 p
Watch my entry, where buyers appear and start to propagate; their
first step is to break the line, as they did correctly. We need to set a
target where we judge whether buyers will continue or end their
momentum: 3-C. We need an idea of when to sell what we just bought. Then
follow the buyers' steps. If something goes wrong before they reach the
target, be ready to move to breakeven, or just run! They don't want to
propagate, and I must protect myself if they don't propagate
properly.

It is amazing to watch the market through a propagation lens: 1-2-3 →
LARGER 1-2-3.

@@b74 p

@@b75 p
Here one can book some profit from the propagation. It is an
intersection with the TP: sellers who believe the market will fall
further sell into this rally, which produces an abnormal reaction (SS).
The selling erases most of the buying gains, and the sellers'
intersection with the return point shows why it is important to take
some profit at the Under, as we did. Sellers who entered at the last
price now feel wrong, because the market no longer acts as before.
Sellers are slow now, and that adds to the buyers' propagation
power.

@@b77 p
Figure: The larger picture: the TP of the leg from the Jumbo top to
the low is reached, as the 1-2-3 grows into a larger 1-2-3. Real data:
GBPJPY, 2-minute, 1–2 March 2022. (The original chart was NZDJPY,
2-minute, 3–7 March 2023; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b78 p
This is the sellers' last chance to add to their orders; otherwise
the bulls are in control, with a small double 6&amp;6 nested in the box.
A buying sign, and buyers re-enter. It is a difficult trade to manage: I
take some profit, but I play for a larger one. Our stop has still not
been hit. Remember, this is a 2-minute development; traders usually go
Inward only for timing, but it is a chance to watch how Goodman theory
works at two minutes. Let's look at the 5-minute.
