@@b1078 h2
Market Correlation (2021)

@@b1079 p
In this paper I look for correlation between currency pairs, and how
it can show us the dominant Matrix. I start with how the mind
correlates, then use Goodman Wave Theory to read a race between two
pairs, EURUSD and GBPUSD.

@@b1080 h3
How the mind correlates

@@b1081 p
Every day, our minds try to correlate what happened in the past, or
just a few days before, to deal with each new challenge. In every
circumstance the mind spends some time, perhaps only 10 seconds,
depending on its state and passion, to correlate and analyse. The aim is
to reach the greatest goal the circumstance allows.

@@b1082 p
That goal can be reached in only three ways: <strong>chance</strong>,
<strong>logic</strong>, or <strong>both</strong> (blessing, or
luck).

@@b1084 p
Figure: Three routes to the goal: chance, logic, and blessing
(luck).

@@b1085 h4
Chance

@@b1086 p
With chance there is zero correlation the mind can work with. With
reason or analysis, or without them, in the end it is decided by
chance.

@@b1087 h4
Logic

@@b1088 p
Logic is based on a true conclusion that correlates with its premise.
The mind can work with a high correlation here. But it meets a lot of
bad fate when the conclusion is false, and it is cold and silent when it
comes to fixing conclusions. Every mind holds a thousand false
conclusions.

@@b1089 h4
Luck, or blessing

@@b1090 p
Luck is built on months and years of practice, as with football
players. The only logic is in the practice: with more practice, a chance
becomes more attainable. But there is no direct cause that achieves the
goal. It is relative logic and relative chance, so the correlations are
relative too. I prefer to call this a blessing rather than luck. It has
a direct link with the law of attraction.

@@b1091 h4
The new theory of chance

@@b1092 p
The new theory of chance states that events with zero correlation can
produce a new correlation across many outcomes. Instead of seeking a
correlation from one coin flip, we find it across 100 flips. There the
mind has a lot to analyse.

@@b1093 p
The greatest inventors in history searched through many outcomes for
a definite correlation that would solve the problem. I do not mean they
used the theory of chance. But in the end, they always investigated any
correlation that could help them reach a goal.

@@b1094 blockquote
<p>"I have not failed. I've just found 10,000 ways that won't work."</p>
<p>"There are no rules here – we're trying to accomplish something."</p>
<p>— Thomas A. Edison</p>

@@b1095 p
From this, I believe there are many correlations between pairs that
help us know the dominant Matrix. We need to know the correlation for
each type of circumstance. Unfortunately, there are no rules: only
investigation, with patience and passion.

@@b1096 h3
Organizing the pairs by the
dollar

@@b1097 p
The forex pairs are many: EURUSD, USDJPY, GBPUSD, USDCHF, AUDUSD,
USDCAD, NZDUSD and more. To me they are a puzzle, a chessboard. With so
many pairs it is easy to lose concentration, so I organize them by
international trade.

@@b1098 blockquote
<p>"In addition to accounting for the bulk of global reserves, the
dollar is the currency of choice for international trade. Major
commodities such as oil are primarily bought and sold using U.S.
dollars."</p>

@@b1099 p
So I take EURUSD, GBPUSD, AUDUSD and NZDUSD. When these pairs rise,
the dollar falls. "If a trader goes long EUR/USD, he or she buys Euros
and sells US dollars."

@@b1100 p
When the dollar falls, the world is in trouble: its external
purchasing power drops. Anyone who saves dollars in a bank finds, over
the years, that they are worth less and less.

@@b1101 p
That is not the purpose of this study. I am looking for a dominant
trend through correlation. But it gives a strong ground to start from.
The market can only move up and down, but with the dollar's purchasing
power falling, these pairs can only move up. That is a logical
correlation: EUR, GBP, AUD and NZD all share at least one direct cause,
the dollar. Here I show a causal analysis using Goodman Theory, which
shows very clearly how purchasing power works at the different Goodman
points, in each circumstance.

The race between EURUSD and
GBPUSD

In both pairs I study each Goodman setup and point, looking for

@@b1108 p
EURUSD shows Dagger behaviour, breaking out (on 26 May in my original
chart). At the same time GBPUSD is faster: Goldilocks behaviour is
faster than a Dagger. The Goldilocks shows that the dollar's purchasing
power has decreased. Together, the Goldilocks and the Dagger are a
decisive sign that the dollar is weak. With two signs, we can go long.
The dollar fails to dominate both EUR and GBP.

@@b1109 p
The two pairs run a human race. I watch it to see which one is faster
and which slower. For now, GBPUSD is faster than EURUSD.

On the larger Propagation, see how well the 3-C works in both. EURUSD

sellers while GBPUSD is still rising. When that happens, anyone trading
GBPUSD must be on alert, because new sellers in EURUSD correlate with
GBPUSD. Goodman traders should take profit and close all long positions.
EURUSD moves down fastest; it gives us the alert.

give the best indication of domination. I showed the Goldilocks and the
Dagger for the buy, and here the same for the sell. It is like a Nested
Overlay, but across two pairs.
