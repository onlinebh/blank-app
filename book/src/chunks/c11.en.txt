Abduction (2023)

@@b746 p
Here are some ideas and applications I do not want to lose. As the
old saying goes: write it, or lose it!

@@b747 p
The chart is like a cat: a different world, a different spirit, a
different mind. The trader needs to connect with that cat by watching it

different conditions.

The story behind the chart

@@b749 p
I first read this on GBPCHF, daily chart; here the same structure is
shown on real EURUSD 1-hour data.

@@b751 p
Figure: Price coiling under a falling line from the excess point and

@@b752 p
There was a propagation (on 16 September 2022, in my original chart).
Then the market coiled between a test point and an excess point, unable
to breach either of them.

@@b753 p
My logical inference about these points is that they are obstacles.
Neither buyers nor sellers can breach them. Watch the volume in March:
many buyers and many sellers struggling at the test point. In April,
buyers could not breach the obstacle of the excess point line. A strong
bear bar changed the equation: a lack of buying momentum. This produced
the last downswing to 29 April.

@@b754 p
This alternation from a buying swing to a selling swing and back to a
buying swing carries a message.

@@b756 p
Figure: The FPS, SS and SPS marked inside the coil, with buyers
turning to propagation mode. Real data: GBPCHF, 1-hour, 14–23 February
2022. (The original chart was GBPCHF, daily, August 2022 – May 2023;
this one shows the same structure on the same symbol, in the window the
data covers.)

@@b757 p
The core of every movement is a 1-2-3, or part of one. The current
price is moving in the SPS of the past Matrix, where buyers are in
propagation mode.

@@b758 h3
Can buyers breach the
obstacle?

@@b759 p
If buyers can propagate within the excess and test levels, how far
can price go? Is there the strength to breach the excess obstacle? The
logical answer is that we are in doubt. Buyers can withdraw from the
market when they struggle with the excess obstacle.

@@b760 p
We need an Overlay that confirms the strength of buying when it faces
the obstacle.

@@b765 p
Goodman is based on matrices, not on time. It is very clear that
larger matrices produce buying. At the SS intersection with the TP,
buying wins. When the sellers' EP of the FPS meets the TP, the sellers
return as an SS.

@@b766 p
A smaller propagation sits within a larger propagation, and the trend
is with the SPS. The failure of the down propagation means buying has
more ease of movement, while sellers have none.

@@b767 p
By trading on the side of propagation, we let the probability of
buying from different matrices work on our side. It is about 52% buying
and 48% selling, and a lower Matrix may change the equation. We must
deal with the risk.

@@b785 h2
Deduction, Induction and
Abduction

@@b786 p
Looking back at these papers, I see that I was really studying one
question: how does a trader reason from what he sees to what he does?
Logic gives three answers, and I used all three.

@@b787 p
<strong>Deduction</strong> goes from a rule to a result. If
everything is a 1-2-3, and I see a 1-2 Setup, then the 3 must follow.
The conclusion is certain, but only as certain as the rule. My four
logical combinations work this way: when the daily and the 1-hour agree
on an extreme, the signal follows from the rule.

@@b788 p
<strong>Induction</strong> goes from many cases to a rule. In the
AUDCAD study I added probability positive causes, #1, #2 and #3, until I
could infer a reversal. This is how we learn the market. But, as I wrote
in "The Problem of Imperfect Induction," the rule is never proven.
Another cause may be at work, and the cause that worked yesterday may
fail tomorrow. Induction gives probability, not certainty.

@@b789 p
<strong>Abduction</strong> goes from a surprising result to the best
explanation. GBPCHF coiled between a test point and an excess point.
Why? My best story was obstacles that neither buyers nor sellers could
breach. Abduction is how we read the story behind the chart, the way one
learns what a cat usually does. It is only plausible, so it must be
tested by an Overlay before we act.

@@b791 p
Figure: The three kinds of reasoning use the same parts, rule, case
and result, in three different directions.

@@b792 p
A good trader moves between all three. Abduction proposes the story.
Deduction tells him what must happen if the story is right. Induction,
case after case, tells him how often it really does. Goodman's words fit
here: the deeper you dig, the fewer possibilities you derive. Each kind
of reasoning removes some possibilities, and what remains is the
trade.
