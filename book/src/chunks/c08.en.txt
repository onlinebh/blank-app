##CTX Part: Rung 2 · Knowing: How Is Truth Learned? | Section: The Logic of Goodman (2022)

@@b489 h3
1.1 Learning the logic of
Goodman

@@b490 p
We learn logic so that we can stand in reality, between probability
and delusion, when we decide. The urgency to learn comes from one fact:
we cannot control what the market does. That inability exists for simple
reasons:

@@b491 ul
<li><p>The market does not exist by our choice.</p></li>
<li><p>Price will not move up or down because of our bias.</p></li>
<li><p>The market exists, but we cannot force it to move as we
wish.</p></li>

@@b492 p
The market continues even if you feel frozen and cannot act. It
continues even if it locks you into a losing position and you panic. It
simply keeps moving normally.

@@b493 p
Logic is our primary tool for becoming aware of this nature, which we
can do nothing about. It produces self-awareness: of the market's
existence, and of the limits of our own ability. So we must be
selective. We must be picky.

@@b494 h4
The process of deciding

@@b495 p
When we face an unknown outcome, we stand between probability and
delusion. This is the furthest the learning curve can take us. There is
no certainty.

@@b496 p
So we must move from the known to the unknown. The known are our
axioms, such as "everything is a 1-2-3". The mind takes this knowledge
and uses it to find the unknowns, and those unknowns are our probability
decisions. It is like algebra, where we use what we know to find X.

@@b497 p
In practice, the trader looks at a timeframe where price is moving.
He knows the problem: will price move to SS or to SPS? Will this action
propagate? He asks several such questions to define the type of
unknown.

@@b498 p
Then his mind starts to work on how to solve that type. He organizes
his knowledge and moves Outward or Inward. He tries to reach a logical
reason for the probable outcome. If, after this process, he can conclude
with confidence where the next direction will be, SPS or SS, the problem
is solved.

@@b499 h4
The five steps

@@b500 p
The process has five steps:

@@b501 blockquote
<p>1. <strong>Know the problem</strong>: the unknown.</p>
<p>2. <strong>Know the type of unknown.</strong> Many traders face the
unknown but do not know what type of unknown it is.</p>
<p>3. <strong>Choose the tool.</strong> By organizing the type of
unknown, we know what we need to use: the Overlay.</p>
<p>4. <strong>Build the evidence.</strong> Using what is known, the
Overlay, we construct logical evidence for what is required to solve the
unknown.</p>
<p>5. <strong>Meet the conclusion.</strong> We match the conclusion of
our logical construction with the unknown.</p>

@@b503 p
Figure: The five steps of a Goodman decision, from knowing the
unknown to the conclusion; experienced traders start at step 3.

@@b504 p
Experienced traders, with some intuition, start directly at steps 3,
4 and 5. For steps 1 and 2 they carry a kind of axiom.

@@b505 p
So the logic of Goodman is a set of research on how to construct an
argument that solves the unknown. It defines the unknown and its type,
and it justifies the tools we use. It gives a reasonable ground for
balancing the Overlay to trigger the trade.

@@b506 h3
1.2 The language of the bar
chart

@@b507 p
Understanding the language of the bar chart helps us receive the
chart's message. Every chart is unique and carries a different
message.

@@b508 p
Logic connects directly with understanding that message. The chart is
only a language, spoken through its basic mediator: the bar, with its
OPEN, CLOSE, HIGH and LOW.

@@b510 p
Figure: GBPUSD, 15-minute, 17–21 February 2022. Every bar is built
from four words, Open, High, Low and Close, and together they record
what buyers and sellers did.

@@b511 p
Logic aims to use at least these four data points to understand the
message clearly. They play a very important role. They bring us the
activity of all buyers and sellers within a specific time.

@@b512 p
The chart does for us something we cannot do. It shows buyers and
sellers from everywhere in the world, and the bar carries their meaning.
This is very deep. We could never watch every buyer and seller in the
real world at every moment. So instead of trying to find out who is
buying and who is selling, we take what the chart already gives us.

@@b513 p
The chart registers all buyer and seller activity. Let me
summarize:

@@b514 blockquote
<p>"The existence of any bar*, exist a meaning about
buyers-sellers."</p>

@@b515 p
That meaning gives us an indication of the trend.

@@b516 p
*In many cases a single bar does not reveal any significant
indication.

@@b517 h4
What is an indication?

@@b518 p
When someone knocks on the door, the mind translates the sound into
an indication: someone is standing in front of the door. That indication
may be significant for us.

@@b519 p
So an indication works like this. We know that something exists, a
sound or a bar. Then, through a process of the mind, it indicates that
something else exists: buyers or sellers.

@@b520 h4
A significant indication

@@b521 p
In the next chart, a significant indication shows the existence of
buyers. Why is it significant? Look at the older bars. Here buyers
increase their energy for the first time. Did such buying energy exist
before? No. That is what makes the indication significant: the existence
of buyers with new energy.

@@b523 p
Figure: EURUSD, 15-minute, 23–25 February 2022. After a long fall,
buyers show new energy at the low on heavy volume: a significant
indication of the future trend.

@@b524 p
A significant indication works as evidence of the future trend.

@@b525 h4
Three types of indication

@@b526 p
The existence of buyers or sellers can carry different meanings. That
is the type of indication. Each type may be significant or
non-significant. There are three types:

@@b527 ul
<li><p>Logical indication</p></li>
<li><p>Character indication</p></li>
<li><p>Structure indication</p></li>

@@b529 p
Figure: The three types of indication (logical, character and
structure), and the three kinds of structure: congruence, implicitly and
intrinsic.

@@b530 h3
Logical indication

@@b531 p
A logical indication is one that has an inherence in existence that
comes from buyers and sellers.

@@b533 p
Figure: EURUSD, 5-minute, 24 February 2022. At A sellers increase
their orders; at B they renew them in the same way.

@@b534 p
At bar A the indication shows inherence to sellers: sellers increase
their orders. At bar B sellers renew their orders, just as at A. Both A
and B carry an inherence.

@@b535 h4
The six types of inherence

@@b536 p
There are six types of inherence. Each applies to both buyers and
sellers. They can show their existence on the chart at any time by:

@@b537 blockquote
<p>1. <strong>Entering</strong> orders into the market.</p>
<p>2. <strong>Leaving</strong> (exiting), that is, stopping to enter
orders into the market.</p>

@@b539 p
Figure: GBPUSD, 5-minute, 24 February 2022. Buyers show their
existence by entering at the low; sellers show theirs by leaving.

@@b540 blockquote
<p>1. <strong>Increasing</strong> their orders to the market.</p>
<p>2. <strong>Decreasing</strong> their orders to the market.</p>

@@b542 p
Figure: GBPUSD, 15-minute, 21–24 February 2022. Buyers increase, then
decrease; sellers increase, then increase more than last time.

@@b543 blockquote
<p>1. <strong>Deciding when</strong> they want to enter orders into the
market.</p>
<p>2. <strong>Deciding why</strong> they want to enter orders into the
market.</p>

@@b545 p
Figure: Buyers and sellers show their existence by when and why they
enter: after the fall, sellers increase at its TP, and again when buyers
rally back to the same price level. Real data: AUDSGD, daily, 19 May
2017 – 13 April 2018. (Same symbol, timeframe and dates as the original
chart. The daily data file has no volume, so the volume panel is
empty.)

@@b546 p
Inherences 1 to 4 also belong to the Character indication. Inherences
5 and 6 work as the Structure indication. In many cases they play
together.

@@b548 p
Figure: Buyers increase at the TP of the rise, then sellers increase
at the TP of the fall, a sign of strong character and of a future trend.
Real data: AUDSGD, daily, 20 November 2017 – 23 March 2018. (Same
symbol, timeframe and dates as the original chart. The daily data file
has no volume, so the volume panel is empty.)

@@b549 p
Here buyers increased with the character of greed. Then sellers
increased, which caused fear in buyers to enter more orders. That is a
significant indication of a future trend.

@@b550 h3
Character indication

@@b551 p
A character indication is one whose inherence comes from the nature
of buyers and sellers: greed and fear.

@@b552 blockquote
<p>"All through time, people have basically acted and re-acted the same
way in the market as a result of: greed, fear, ignorance, and hope-that
is why the numerical formations and patterns recur on a constant basis."
— Jesse Livermore</p>

@@b553 p
Crying, for example, is an indication that someone feels pain. As I
said, character has four essential inherences. Extracting indications
from natural inherence is studied by different schools of psychology and
philosophy, and the door is always open to further study.

@@b554 h4
Entering and
exiting: the responsibility of choice

@@b555 p
The first two inherences are:

@@b556 blockquote
<p>1. They show their existence on the chart by entering orders into the
market at any time.</p>
<p>2. They show their existence on the chart by leaving (exiting) the
market at any time.</p>

@@b557 p
Both show that traders have a choice: to enter or to exit. They carry
full responsibility for those choices. Their choices run in the market,
where some win and others lose.

@@b558 p
All choices move toward their destinations, sometimes under
predestination. In the end, our control is limited to the choice.
Everything returns to choices.

@@b559 p
A good trade returns good profit. But that is not always the case: a
bad trade returns a loss. Because there are consequences, participants
want to make rational choices. Yet humans act non-rationally, through
their beliefs.

@@b561 p
Figure: EURNZD, 5-minute, 24 February 2022. Buyers enter at the top
and many become losers; sellers enter at the bottom. Every move is the
result of a choice.

@@b562 h4
Increasing and
decreasing: hope and anxiety

@@b563 p
The next two inherences are:

@@b564 blockquote
<p>1. They show their existence on the chart by increasing orders to the
market at any time.</p>
<p>2. They show their existence on the chart by decreasing orders to the
market at any time.</p>

@@b565 p
When traders realize they are against the trend, they enter a state
of hope that they can still make some profit. That hope increases their
orders. Then anxiety from the losing side makes them decrease their
orders.

@@b567 p
Figure: EURNZD, 5-minute, 24 February 2022. Above the line buyers
keep adding orders in hope, while sellers who sold the bottom grow
anxious.
