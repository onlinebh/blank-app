Defining Volume from the
Ground Up

@@b2052 p
Where could I start? Any first sign would help. I began by analyzing
the dictionary definition. The Oxford dictionary says volume is "the
amount of something". That was enough for me. I wanted a simple model to
work with before I added any more parameters.

@@b2053 p
I asked two simple questions:

@@b2054 blockquote
<p>1. What benefit do I get from knowing the amount of something?</p>
<p>2. What type of amount is it?</p>

@@b2055 h4
The benefit of knowing an
amount

@@b2056 p
I remember a physics class about units. Before modern units, people
used very old tools to measure amounts.

@@b2058 p
Figure: Old units of land measure (oxgang, virgate, carucate) — every
age measured amounts with its own tools.

@@b2059 p
The benefit of knowing an amount is obvious: it lets us put things in
the right place. How? An amount gives a differential picture. From that
difference we can find the level or boundary. It is a matter of
judgment: who is taller, who is shorter? Our ability to judge depends on
our experience and knowledge.

@@b2060 p
Take chemistry. Which atom has the largest atomic radius? Potassium.
We do not know everything, so we cannot judge everything. Sometimes we
need a special tool to measure. For example:

@@b2061 blockquote
<p>"This is possible because carbon atoms occur in heavy and light forms
or isotopes, and measuring the relative amounts of each can reveal the
source of the carbon. Using carbon isotopes in this way is not a new
idea, but it requires extremely precise—and expensive—measurements."</p>

@@b2062 h4
My simple conclusion

@@b2063 p
From this analysis I drew four points:

@@b2064 ul
<li><p>The main benefit of knowing an amount is to put each thing at its
proper level or boundary.</p></li>
<li><p>Some types of amount are easy to judge; others are hard.</p></li>
<li><p>Judgment aims to place the amount inside its boundary — no more,
no less. Sometimes the judgment is relative, as with carbon.</p></li>
<li><p>The ways of measuring differ: different units, different tools,
different devices.</p></li>

@@b2065 p
I use this simple conclusion as the ground on which to build
something bigger. The word "volume" alone is not enough. We must dig
deeper, because the model works only according to what we put into
it.

@@b2066 h3
Measuring Humans

@@b2067 p
In the stock market, the reason behind every movement is humans, and
only humans. I know of no monkey or animal that buys or sells. If anyone
denies it, I welcome the discussion 😊.

@@b2068 p
So the critical question is not "how do we measure the number of
humans?" It is "how do we put human action into levels or
boundaries?"

@@b2069 p
Old wisdom shows how hard this is. Socrates said: "I know that I am
intelligent because I know that I know nothing." There, intelligence
goes with knowing nothing. Today it is the opposite: intelligence goes
with the one who knows everything. The old teacher said: "I cannot teach
anybody anything. I can only make them think." Today we say: go to
school and take a certificate. I am not judging which is right. I only
want to show how difficult it is to build a unit that measures human
action or habit. From here my other ideas were born — energy, passion
and others.

@@b2070 p
Still, some human things are easy to judge, such as love and hate.
Someone who loves another will not kill him — that is 99% sure. So in
some cases we can judge with high confidence.

@@b2071 p
But can we measure it with an equation? It is impossible. Can anyone
write an equation that tells whether there is love in his marriage? We
have tools inside our brain that measure the amount of love easily. Can
any equation replace testosterone? A monitor may tell you how healthy
your testosterone is, but reading a description is not the same as
living the feeling. This is why I do not believe in indicators. I may be
100% wrong, but I cannot fight my own analysis without a sign that
proves me wrong.

@@b2072 h3
My Definition:
The Amount of Selling at a Price

@@b2073 p
So what does volume in the market measure? It measures the amount of
change in property. Mr. Goodman was right: price causes volume. In every
market, the amount of selling depends on what the price is.

@@b2074 p
My definition is therefore: <strong>volume is the amount of selling,
depending on the price.</strong> When the market is at price X, it
generates an amount of selling Y.

@@b2075 h4
What the amount tells us

@@b2076 p
What is the benefit? In every market, the amount of selling tells a
story about price:

@@b2077 ul
<li><p>When the amount of selling increases, the price will probably
rise.</p></li>
<li><p>When the amount of selling decreases, the price will probably be
discounted.</p></li>
<li><p>When the amount of selling runs at average, the price will
probably follow whoever dominates.</p></li>
<li><p>When the amount of selling is abnormally high, the price is
probably at a critical point.</p></li>
<li><p>When the amount of selling is abnormally low, the price is also
probably at a critical point. No business can live without customers —
the real boss of every business is the customer.</p></li>

@@b2079 p
Figure: Reading the amount of selling: what increasing, decreasing,
average and abnormal volume probably tell us about price.

@@b2080 h4
A big note

@@b2081 p
One bar is not enough to tell the story, and the same is true of
volume. We must work Inward and Outward to understand the amount of
selling in each hour, day and week. Sometimes the market moves up on a
very low amount of selling simply because it has just come from an
abnormal amount. This is a soft judgment.

@@b2083 p
Figure: Volume bars read as nothing more than the quantity of sales
in each period. Real data: GBPUSD, 1-hour volume, 28 Feb – 4 Mar 2022.
(The original was a volume strip cut from another chart; this one is
drawn from real volume.)

@@b2084 h3
Reading Volume with the
Matrix

@@b2085 p
So all these columns follow one simple path: the quantity of sales.
By judging the quantity, we can put it in the right place. For example,
when the market moves up on a very low quantity of sales, we can easily
judge that the market will probably go down. It is just an Overlay.

@@b2086 p
How do we judge that the quantity is very low? It is not hard:
memorize the Matrix. Thank you, Mr. Goodman. I watch each Matrix,
memorize the change, and make my judgment behind it. Most of the time I
combine it with a judgment of time. Time represents control, like how
long a football team keeps the ball.

@@b2087 p
The change in the amount of sales in each FPS, SS and SPS can tell
the story.

@@b2089 p
Figure: Volume inside a down 1-2-3: the amount of selling under the
FPS, SS and SPS, and what each change means.

@@b2090 h4
Example: EURUSD

@@b2092 p
Figure: A 1-2-3 down with the volume under each leg marked 1, 2 and
3. Real data: EURUSD, 4-hour, 17 Feb – 2 Mar 2022. (The original chart
was EURUSD daily, July–August 2021; this one shows the same
structure.)

@@b2093 blockquote
<p>1. <strong>FPS (1).</strong> The volume fits the range of the wave
very well; everything is fine. The amount of sales at 1 shows how the
down move travels at an average pace. I memorize that.</p>
<p>2. <strong>SS (2).</strong> But everything can change. At 2 the
amount of sales is clearly lower than at 1. It shows only a correction.
The time is very short, so there is no control.</p>
<p>3. <strong>SPS (3).</strong> Here we have an indication. The volume
is abnormal for the range and for the time held. There are a lot of
sales and the market is at a critical point. Because sales increased on
a down SPS held for very little time, it shows many buyers want to enter
the market.</p>
<p>4. <strong>After the SPS.</strong> As soon as the SPS ends, the
quantity of sales rises above the SS at 2. More buyers want to bid the
market. Reading this, I know the right place for this amount: it is to
raise the price. Buyers have a lot of energy to pull the market up.</p>

@@b2094 p
I know this is tick volume, but it works well with judgment behind
the Matrix. Reading volume without the 1-2-3 will mislead you a lot.

@@b2095 p
That is my very simple study of how I read volume — the quantity of
sales. Thanks for reading.
