Part two: what the
weekly reports recorded

@@b3437 p
Each weekly report was a workbook. The HOME sheet gave the week's
polling summary: the percentage of professionals and of amateurs bullish
on each pair. Every pair then had its own sheet holding the full weekly
history of both readings.

@@b3438 p
The pairs covered were the eighteen in the method: AUDCAD, AUDJPY,
AUDNZD, AUDUSD, CHFJPY, EURAUD, EURCAD, EURCHF, EURGBP, EURJPY, EURUSD,
GBPCHF, GBPJPY, GBPUSD, NZDUSD, USDCAD, USDCHF and USDJPY. By 2023 the
reports also tracked crude oil (CL), gold (AU), the S&amp;P 500 (SPX)
and Bitcoin (BTCUSD), and the last report added the 10-year Treasury
note (TY10).

@@b3439 p
The reports I kept run from the week of 1 January 2023 to the week of
8 July 2023. The history inside them starts on 4 September 2022, so the
last workbook holds 44 weekly polls per pair.

What We Built Together
(2024–2026)

@@b3446 p
For six years Michael wrote and I drew. That is the shortest true
account of our partnership. Michael carried the method in his head,
eleven years of it with Charlie, and the words came to him easily. The
diagrams did not. I had the patience for the diagrams, and over time I
took on the rest of the work around a book: the layout, the covers, the
website, the courses. This chapter records what we made, who did what,
and what each piece meant to me.

The Goodman
Trader's Playbook — the Molokai Setup

@@b3450 p
The Playbook is a cut-to-the-chase manual for finding and trading one
Goodman setup, the Molokai. Michael wrote the text. I drew the diagrams,
rebuilt the charts and designed the cover, and in the end I organised
the manuscript for the printer. It is the one book we put both our names
to.

@@b3451 p
It started as an old idea of his that had stalled for one reason. On
2024-06-18 he wrote: <em>"I started the Goodman Playbook a couple of
times...the issue was doing all the diagrams and charts; the text not
too difficult."</em> I answered the next day: <em>"Still i like the
playbook idea."</em> A year later, on 2025-06-27, the problem was the
same: <em>"I enjoy the writing but not the
diagrams/charts/figures..."</em>

@@b3452 p
The name came in July 2025. On 2025-07-16 he wrote: <em>"I hope
Charlie not upset I rename 6 &amp; 6 to Molokai."</em> Molokai is the
Hawaiian island where he spent his honeymoon, and the setup carries that
memory. In the introduction he put it plainly: <em>"For the record, 6
&amp; 6 refers to 'six swings and six parameters.'"</em>

@@b3453 p
On 2025-09-12 he asked: <em>"Almost done with Playbook! I'll need you
to redo several charts, diagrams, etc... maybe design nice cover
also??"</em> By November the count was fifty-two diagrams. On
2026-01-26, after he saw my chapter 3 and 4 drawings, he wrote:
<em>"yours so muchore professional looking!!!"</em> On 2026-09-07 I
wrote back: <em>"Let's finish the Playbook. I organize it, update the
diagrams."</em>

@@b3454 p
What it meant: the Playbook proved the division of labour worked. He
no longer had to explain a figure for longer than it took to draw it. I
drew it, he checked it, and the book moved.

Goodman for Grasshoppers

@@b3460 p
Grasshoppers is Michael's introductory book, in its third edition
since 2023. It is named for his Hong Kong student, the one he called
Grasshopper, whose sayings run through it. On 2025-01-16 I quoted one
back to him: <em>"GRASSHOPPER SAYS: You Learn Goodman the using the same
process as you Trade."</em> The book is his. My part was the new cover,
built on its own figure, the Goodman Smoothie: large degree narrowing
down to the trade.

@@b3461 p
What it meant: Grasshoppers is where a new student starts, and the
Smoothie is the right picture for that. You work down from the large
degree to the trade.

@@b3463 p

exceeding its own levels, one correction at a time. I read it many
times. On 2025-02-08 I wrote: <em>"Re- Reading Over the over.
Article."</em> On 2025-07-18 he told me: <em>"I'm going to have to
completely rewrite over the over - too out of date to just edit."</em> I
replied the same day: <em>"Over the over very useful article. Last time

transferred vertex by vertex from his figure.

@@b3464 p
What it meant: this was the paper that taught me how to read a Line,
