2025: expect the worst

@@b2714 p
In January 2025 I described four kinds of equity periods: a rare best
streak, a worst streak of maybe 10 losses in a row, a rare run of trades
near BE, and the normal days, the stairs of one step up and a little
down that Charlie called 10-4-1. If I want a maximum drawdown of 2.5%,
the simplest way is to size so that 10 losses in a row still fit.
"Knowing the boundaries decreasing the surprising element .. its about
surviving first . The goal is BE.!" (January 24, 2025)

2025: thirty souls

@@b2717 p
In March 2025 the idea found its final form, and its name:

@@b2718 p
"Keep in mind we are trading the money not the market. so, every
trade is part of 30 trades, find the best or simply no action. you do
not have many souls in the market, have only 30 souls! take the best to
survive after 30 trades." (March 5, 2025)

@@b2719 p
Thirty souls means each trade spends one life. I do not spend a life
on a setup I only half believe. The first goal of the series is to
survive it, at least at breakeven. Profit comes from the probability
being on my side over the whole series, not from any one trade.

What the arithmetic
actually looks like

@@b2723 p
I wanted to see my rule run, not only describe it. So we simulated
it. The edge is the one I used as an example in my letters: 40% winners
at two times the risk, 60% losers at one. The size is my rule: a 5%
maximum drawdown divided by 30, which is about 0.17% per trade.

@@b2724 p
Over 10,000 simulated series of 30 trades, not one touched the 5%
limit. The median series ended about 1% up. Around three in ten ended
slightly down, which is honest: 30 trades is a short road. But nobody
died.

@@b2725 p
Then the same edge at higher sizes, over 100 trades. At 1% per trade,
a drawdown of 20% from the peak happened in about 2% of runs. At 1.5%,
in about 16%. At 2%, in about 41%. At 3%, in about 79%. Same system,
same win rate, same reward. Only the size changed.

@@b2727 p
Figure: A simulation of my max-DD/30 rule (illustration, not a
forecast), with simulated risk of ruin at higher sizes.

@@b2728 p
This is the whole chapter in one picture. The edge is the engine. The
size decides whether you are still driving when the edge pays.

Expectancy as momentum

@@b2730 p
In July 2025, after our talks about Molokai, I tried to write the
same thing as a line. There are two kinds of curves close to
compounding: "Y = kx" and "Y = k/x." In one, things multiply and riches
come to the door. In the other, things divide and poverty comes to the
door.

@@b2731 p
With my example edge: 0.4 times 2, minus 0.6 times 1, gives 0.2. "0.2
is the factor. K . While x is money." And then the advice to myself:
"You don't need to modify the factor. Just keep it consistently. But add
more money slowly" (July 18, 2025)

@@b2732 p
Keep k steady by keeping the Golden Ratio healthy. Grow x slowly. In
December 2024 I had already said I like to trade at a risk of ruin of
around 0 to 5% in my simulations. The momentum line is the same idea,
seen from the other side.

What I learned

@@b2741 p
From 1.5% in 2021 to thirty souls in 2025, the rules grew more
patient and more humble. They moved from "how much can I risk?" to "how
much can I survive?" The arithmetic is not exciting. It is what lets the
exciting part happen again tomorrow.

The
Error-Proof Trader: Poka-Yoke in Trading (2026)

@@b2743 p
Thirty Souls is about the arithmetic of survival. This chapter is
about the engineering of it. The arithmetic tells me how much I can
lose. The engineering makes sure I cannot lose it by mistake.

@@b2744 h3
Mistakes are an operations
risk

@@b2745 p
On 2026-04-03 I wrote to Michael that trading needs three things
aligned: the dynamic market, the trading system, and yourself.

@@b2746 blockquote
<p>"If have a system not aligned with market means losing. If have a
good system deals with market but unable to be discipline with means
also losing. Making mistake is a operations risk to the system."</p>

@@b2747 p
My background in risk work taught me to separate risks by where they
come from. Market risk is the price going against a good setup. I accept
it, because it is part of the 10-4-1. Operations risk is different. It
is the trade without a stop, the lot typed wrong, the revenge entry
after a loss. Years ago I defined it simply: "Mistakes = any
controllable action in wrong time." Market risk I manage. Operations
risk I should remove.

@@b2748 h3
Finding the causes first

@@b2749 p
Before you can block a mistake, you have to know where it comes from.
On 2026-02-03 I wrote:

@@b2750 blockquote
<p>"the smaller risk if avoided will generate larger risk! . don't think
about profit , profits that's what we want , but if not protect the
process will lead to big loss. i like fishbone diagram"</p>

@@b2751 p
The fishbone diagram is a quality tool. You write the problem at the
head, a bad trade, and draw the bones behind it: method, mind, size,
market, review. Each bone holds the causes. When I draw it for my own
losses, most of the bones are not about the market at all. They are
about me.

@@b2752 p
The day before, on 2026-02-02, I wrote the questions I ask before I
accept a trade:

@@b2753 blockquote
<p>"1)how could the setup fails? 2)at which overlays i rely and what the
sign's tell me wrong about the combination. Then 3) what the impact when
i am wrong. Its too high? 4) where is the causal factor? Have
whitespace? Its clean?"</p>

@@b2754 p
And the rule for acceptance: "Logically the risk accept when 1) the
impact of risk its very low impact that it's fine to live with. 2) the
risk is very low compare to profit." This is a risk register for one
trade. The failure modes are named before money is at stake.

@@b2755 h3
Measuring the mind

@@b2756 p
A mistake starts in a state of mind, so I measure the state. On
2026-03-17 I sent Michael a table for rating emotion from 1 to 10 at
each checkpoint: before the trade, at entry, during it, at exit, after
it, and at random moments when I am only watching. The scale is simple.
1–2 is very angry or frustrated. 3–4 is nervous or uncomfortable. 5–6 is
calm and in control. 7–8 is confident and energized. 9–10 is too excited
or overconfident.

@@b2757 p
The point is in the two ends. A 9 is as dangerous as a 2. The best
trading happens in the middle, at 5 or 6. As I wrote under the table:
"Monitoring your emotions helps keep them under control."
