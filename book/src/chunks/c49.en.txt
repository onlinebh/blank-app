Part 4: A Map for Every
Market

@@b3132 p
What about the other forex pairs? My goal is to build an overlay for
each market, as I did for these four pairs. Here is my diagram.

@@b3133 ul
<li><p>Any pair that does not follow its market, or moves by itself with
no clear correlation, I avoid and mark with an X.</p></li>
<li><p>Any pair that leads its market, I use as the market's flag: a red
flag when down, a green flag when up.</p></li>

@@b3134 p
For example, USD has a red flag, CHF a red flag and JPY a red flag,
while NZD has a green flag. So this time I avoid all buys and only sell,
as the red flags in the laydowns for all markets show.

@@b3135 p
I enter the market only when there are two confirmations. For
example: a Goldilocks in AUDUSD and a Goldilocks in EURUSD. Then I enter
a trade, shown on the map by a small bulb, which means both have a
Setup. The more Setups in one market, the more strength. It means the
market is ready to sell.

@@b3136 p
I also add a mark to show which markets are fastest to weaken. Here,
a green mark sits behind the USD pairs.

@@b3138 p
Figure: A laydown for all markets: one map per currency group (USD,
JPY, NZD, CHF), with flags for the leader and bulbs where two Setups
confirm.

@@b3139 p
My job is only to draw a laydown for each of them. It takes around
one hour a day. This system works best on a large time frame such as
daily or weekly. In this study I made the market laydown only for the
USD pairs.

@@b3140 p
From it I can know when the market is best to trade. As shown here,
the USD markets are best when they turn into a Dagger. I use the laydown
as a timing tool. For example, I know I need around several weeks before
the market prepares to move up.
