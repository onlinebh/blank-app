18. The lake and the bathtub

@@b2810 p
<em>A Goodmanism Michael had forgotten for fifty years.</em>

@@b2811 p
Michael remembered this while walking one of his dogs around a small
lake, when he saw a man with a metal detector searching the shore. "I
have not thought of this for a LONG time. That tells me there are
probably still some Goodmanisms and Charlie stories locked up in my poor
old brain!"

@@b2812 p
One morning at Peavey, one of the younger traders was having no
success. He had tried this and that and now announced, "Charlie, I've
decided instead of studying all these markets I'm going to concentrate
on just two markets!"

@@b2813 p
Charlie replied: "If you can't find water in a lake, how do you
expect to find it in a bathtub?"

@@b2815 p
Figure: If there is no water in the lake of all markets, narrowing to
a bathtub of two will not find any.

@@b2816 p
Michael wrote that he laughs on and off for half an hour every time
he thinks of it. When someone at home asked why, he said, "Fifty years
ago I can still hear Charlie's voice and see his smile. It makes me
happy and brings peace to my mind!"

@@b2817 p
<strong>What it means.</strong> Narrowing your focus does not fix a
method that does not work. First find an edge, then narrow.

@@b2818 p
<strong>Try this.</strong> Write down what you believe your edge is,
in one sentence. If you cannot, simplifying will not help.

19. When we enter someone
else's house

@@b2820 p
<em>Olives in the spaghetti.</em>

@@b2821 p
Michael introduces this one as impossible to unpack until you hear
the story. One of Dave Downs' bankers was giving a birthday party for
his granddaughter, who wanted a big party. He told Dave to bring
friends. So Dave invited Michael and Charlie and brought his own
ten-year-old granddaughter. The four of them drove to Cherry Hills
Farms, an area of big houses on large lots. The banker's house was at
least 12,000 square feet on two acres.

@@b2822 p
They peeked into the kitchen, which was the size of a small house
itself and a frenzy of activity. Near them someone was mixing a can of
olives into a large bowl of spaghetti. Dave's granddaughter whispered,
with great certainty, "Grandpa, you don't put olives in spaghetti!"

@@b2823 p
Charlie took Michael aside and said, "When we enter someone else's
house, we are all children."

@@b2824 p
Michael's first reading was that you must be deferential in someone
else's domain. "Close, but Charlie's take was much deeper." Each of us
learns to do routine things in a certain way and forgets there are other
ways to do the same task. The girl had never seen olives in spaghetti,
so she thought it was wrong. We do the same when we meet a different way
of doing things, and reject it out of hand.

@@b2825 p
Decades later Michael heard a child say, "That's not the way you make
bread!" and the phrase came straight back to him.

@@b2826 p
<strong>What it means.</strong> When you meet a method, a culture or
a person that does it differently, be a student first.

@@b2827 p
<strong>Try this.</strong> This week, when you disagree with another
trader's approach, ask them to show you one trade from start to finish
before you say anything.

@@b2828 h2
20. Do you wrap up your poop?

@@b2829 p
<em>The Petroleum Club, 1970s.</em>

@@b2830 p
Jerry Peters, the Bache broker, took Michael and Charlie to lunch at
the Petroleum Club in Denver, a plush penthouse for oil men. It was the
1970s oil boom and everyone was making easy cash. At the next table,
three or four oil men were splitting the proceeds of a deal. They began
arguing loudly about how much each should get.

@@b2831 p
Charlie said: "They act like they are going to take it with them when
they die. Money is the last thing you want in the next world! Do you
wrap up your poop and take it with you on vacation?"

@@b2832 p
Michael calls it one of his favorites. He says Charlie had a special,
unique mind.

@@b2833 p
<strong>What it means.</strong> If you cannot take it with you, then
how you hold it matters more than how much you hold.

@@b2834 p
<strong>Try this.</strong> Ask yourself what the money in your
account is for. Write the answer on the first page of your trading
journal.

@@b2835 h2
Our Conversations on Value

@@b2836 p
Charlie's stories about doing right were never only about Charlie.
Over six years of letters, Michael and I kept returning to the same
question from different doors: what is knowledge worth, who should pay
for it, and what does a teacher owe the world? Michael had a short, sad,
funny name for the problem. He called it "Pearls -&gt; Pork Chops." You
give pearls; people treat them like a plate of pork chops, or walk past
them altogether. This chapter collects our exchanges on that theme. I
have put his words first, then mine, and then what I think now.

@@b2837 h3
Pearls -&gt; Pork Chops

@@b2838 p
Michael, 2024-05-07, as he began posting free articles on
Substack:

@@b2839 blockquote
<p>"Years ago I asked to give full seminar on Goodman. I prepare two
weeks, long ppt I think close to 200 slides!... About half-way through
first session on Basic Principles everyone has blank look on faces. I
hear Charlie, 'Dad, these people are here for a free lunch and you just
told them there's no such thing as a free lunch!' Think same with SubS
but try for a few weeks."</p>

@@b2840 p
A week later, 2024-05-15: "Half-way done with this 'experiment'...
Pearls -&gt; Pork Chops. :( Golden Ratios post should have 10k read
ahaha."

@@b2841 p
My reply on the Golden Ratios article, 2024-05-09:

@@b2842 blockquote
<p>"Amazing article. I read it many times to absorb. Golden ratio
assessment every 10 trades. Average win / average loss. Play as a
probability of anticipation for the next 10 trades... Wow."</p>

@@b2843 p
<em>Reflection.</em> The number of readers was small, but the depth
of the reading was not. I read that article again and again, and it
changed how I review my own trades. A teacher counts heads; a student
counts what stayed in his head. Both counts are real. Michael's
disappointment was honest, and I never tried to argue him out of it, but
I hope this book is one small proof that the pearls did not all land in
the mud.

@@b2844 h3
Free Versus Paid

@@b2845 p
Michael, 2024-06-20, planning the Playbook:

@@b2846 blockquote
<p>"Will not be free like SubS. I learn long ago if people don't pay
they don't take it seriously, see value. 'Free' they think is junk."</p>

@@b2847 p
And again, 2025-07-31, about his work on Abduction, which he shared
with only a few students. He passed on advice he had been given years
before:

@@b2848 blockquote
<p>"If you give free people will think worthless, not respect it. The
more you charge the more they appreciate it! Good advice."</p>

@@b2849 p
My answer came months later, 2025-12-22, when we were planning how to
offer the material:

@@b2850 blockquote
<p>"A mini course? Only if you have time for a few students, with a note
'by a reservation, only for serious students.' ... I am happy to be a
Goodman mentor; teaching Charlie's ideas make me alive. Lucky I get a
credit from you to teach the wisdom that I receive."</p>

@@b2851 p
<em>Reflection.</em> A price is not only money; it is a filter for
seriousness. The student who pays, in money or in effort, has already
decided that the thing has value, and that decision is half of learning.
Michael did not become less generous when he chose to charge. He became
more careful about where his generosity would grow. I took the same
lesson into my own courses: give the door freely, but ask for commitment
before the inner rooms.

@@b2852 h3
Greed and the Method

@@b2853 p
Michael, 2023-12-09, closing a funny story about money he had hidden
and lost:

@@b2854 blockquote
<p>"Greed eventually make you very poor. :)"</p>

@@b2855 p
My replies, 2023-12-09 and 2023-12-10:

@@b2856 blockquote
<p>"Still I remember Charlie, when he say: If I am greed the system will
not work, and I lose."</p>
<p>"Yes. Greed equal to poor."</p>

@@b2857 p
Michael returned to it from another angle, 2025-07-28, on what
changed in trading in the 1970s:

@@b2858 blockquote
<p>"The 'free lunch' movement in trading began in the early to mid
1970s... The technical analysis work before then always also required
some work and judgement... It started the trend of just plugging numbers
into equations and expecting profits to get spit out the other end."</p>

@@b2859 p
My reply the same day:

@@b2860 blockquote
<p>"Trading is process starting from understanding the risk that relates
with the setup, then the importance to be focus to find a
high-percentage setups. Then sitting, manage the trade until claiming
the rewards. Finally don't count the chickens; instead do many bathtub
analysis for every 30 trades."</p>

@@b2861 p
<em>Reflection.</em> Greed and the free lunch are the same hunger
seen from two sides. The greedy trader wants more than the market
offers; the free-lunch trader wants it without the work. The Goodman
method asks for the opposite on both counts: take only what the setup
gives, and do the work of reading the chart yourself. That is why it
cannot be sold to someone who wants a slot machine, and why it rewards
the patient so well.
