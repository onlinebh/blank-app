Market Adventure (2021)

@@b3028 p
In this study I follow four pairs on the daily chart until I find a
trade candidate: EURUSD, AUDUSD, GBPUSD and NZDUSD. I take 14 April 2021
as "today". There was nothing special about that day; I chose it at
random. The aim is to build a campaign that helps us grow our capital,
and to show how 1-2-3s, intersections, volume, correlation and laydowns
fit together. For this book each pair's daily structure is redrawn on
real 2022 data, sometimes from another pair; each caption names the data
shown, and the dates in the text are those of my original charts.

@@b3032 h4
Reading the chart as an
equilibrium

@@b3033 p
My first objective is to look at the chart as what I call a Nash
equilibrium. It explains where buyers reach their maximum, and where
sellers reach theirs, at the best option each side has.

@@b3034 p
I look at the efforts of buyers and sellers as three steps: the
1-2-3. The 1-2-3 is the basic component that buyers and sellers must
follow. In Goodman it is an axiom. So let me show how the buyers'
efforts made a 1-2-3.

@@b3035 p
Because we are dealing with the market's past, before the current
day, I think it is best to draw a larger 1-2-3. My focus is on the end
of each maximum effort of buyers and sellers in the race.

@@b3037 p
Figure: The large 1-2-3: swing 1 runs up from the low to point 1,
then swing 2 corrects down to point 2. Real data: NZDUSD, 1-hour, 14–24
February 2022. (The original chart was EURUSD daily, January 2020 to 14
April 2021; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b3038 h4
Jumbo 1

@@b3039 p
The market shows a Jumbo 1, a large swing 1. On my original EURUSD
daily chart, its BP is on 20 March 2020 and its EP on 6 January 2021.
Hope dominated the market for more than six months. It shows how strong
the buyers were in the past.

@@b3040 h4
Intersections instead of
Fibonacci

@@b3041 p
Before negotiating with swing 2, I look at how intersections work in
Goodman. In Goodman we prefer an intersection to a Fibonacci number.

@@b3042 p
In brief, an intersection is where buyers and sellers struggled in
the past. Watch it like a history film: that location was the deadliest
conflict of the past. In Goodman we call it a Return Zone. Swing 2
returns to that deadliest intersection, and this phenomenon can happen
on every time frame.

@@b3043 p
A bonus: when the zone holds a sideways movement for more than a few
weeks, it is stronger. That makes it more likely that swing 2 stops at
that zone.

@@b3044 p
So I ask a question. At which price level in swing 1 did buyers
struggle with sellers for a long time, fighting there again and again?
Can you split swing 1 into its own 1-2-3?

@@b3046 p
Figure: Swing 1 split into its own 1-2-3; the box marks the struggle
inside swing 1, and EP is the low that ends it. Real data: NZDUSD,
1-hour, 14–24 February 2022. (The original chart was EURUSD daily,
January 2020 to 14 April 2021; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b3047 p
Buyers struggled with sellers for weeks in the box zone. Swing 2
stopped exactly between the EP-FPS and the EP-SS. When swing 2
intersects an old EP-SS, there is a high probability that sellers are
losing their power. Buyers take control of the trend again at 2 and walk
into swing 3.

I watch all these conflict levels to see where swing 3 will most
probably stop. One can also make a 3-C count for every 1-2-3 inside
swing 3 to find its end, or use market-environment analysis. In the end,
the behavior (price action) in swing 3 will show us where it stops.

@@b3065 p
When the market cannot complete a 1-2-3 cycle, it shows more
weakness. So I watch this carefully.

@@b3066 h4
Volume inside the boxes

@@b3067 p
Let me clean the chart to study the volume action. Volume represents
the amount of trading at each price level. Read together with the range
of each box, it gives a good indication.

@@b3069 p
Figure: Box A around swing 1, a second box around swing 2, and the
volume marked at A, 1 and 2. Real data: NZDUSD, 1-hour, 14–24 February
2022. (The original chart was EURUSD daily, January 2020 to 14 April
2021; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b3070 ul
<li><p><strong>At 1:</strong> a lot of volume (new buyers), and the
range of box A is very high. Buyers at 1 had great power to achieve such
a range.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>At 2:</strong> relatively less volume at the start of the
SS (the box). Sellers controlled the SS with a small amount of trading.
This shows buyers still in control; the uptrend is still valid.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>At 3:</strong> the SPS starts with no buyers. This is a
symptom. Buyers move up on a very small amount, which shows they are
leaving the market; they do not want this price. If large buyers are not
invited into this swing, sellers can easily dominate the trend.</p></li>

@@b3071 p
No wonder: Jumbo 1 moved for more than six months, and buyers lost
their energy. Swing 3 can show the next trend very clearly. So on day 14
I leave EURUSD without any trade. Keeping cash is better than
trading.

@@b3072 h4
Spread triple

@@b3073 p
Sometimes the volume is not clear, so I use the spread triple instead
to measure the power in each cycle.

@@b3075 p
Figure: Spread triple: a jump, a back, a second jump, then a failed
cycle and a big back below both trend lines. Real data: AUDUSD, 1-hour,
14–24 February 2022. (The original chart was EURUSD daily, 2021; this
one shows the same structure.)

@@b3076 p
After the last jump, EURUSD (shown here on AUDUSD data) came back and
failed to complete a cycle, to move higher than the last jump. This
shows weakness in the buyers, while the sellers pushed further back than
at any earlier time.

@@b3077 h4
EURUSD conclusion

@@b3078 p
In my opinion EURUSD cannot move back up to 1, because:

@@b3079 blockquote
<p>1. Jumbo 1 took more than six months, and that amount of time
destroyed the demand.</p>
<p>2. At 2, sellers moved on very small volume, as the volume shows.</p>
<p>3. The cycles are incomplete, because the market moves without
buyers. Compare the buyers at 1 (the BP of the Jumbo) with the buyers at
2.</p>

@@b3080 p
I also do not think the market will move below the BP of the Jumbo.
Wave 2 showed no great amount of sellers, no LARGE SELLERS. It is just
that buyers no longer want to enjoy the market. When the SPS moves down,
it will test 2 again and again, trying to break it.

@@b3081 p
So I think the market will follow this laydown: an incomplete cycle
and a sell that stops before the BP of the Jumbo (see the EURUSD panel
of the laydown plate below). We can always change a laydown when the
chart changes. A laydown is a vision of the future of the pair. It is
better to move one step at a time and wait for the market to confirm it.
When the market confirms my laydown, I trade. Otherwise I draw another
laydown and let the market confirm it for me.
