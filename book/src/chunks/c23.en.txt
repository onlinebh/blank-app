@@b1479 h3
The idea: think in the
frame of the 1-2-3

@@b1480 p
The Goodman Box is simple. Instead of thinking in the 1-2-3, we think
in the frame of the 1-2-3. We frame every 1-2-3, and then we deal only
with its two edges: the EP-SPS at the top and the BP-FPS at the
bottom.

@@b1481 p
So this study deals with every frame that contains a 1-2-3. Once the
1-2-3 is framed, we forget about it and concentrate our mind on the
frame alone: EP-SPS and BP-FPS.

@@b1483 p
Figure: A 1-2-3 framed by a box, read only at its upper edge (EP-SPS)
and lower edge (BP-FPS), with the terms Excess, TP and TEST.

@@b1484 p
At this beginning stage I study only these relationships. I will not
go further into angle, time or volume. I only want to show how to
interpret boxes within a market context. In Goodman we know the market
moves by 1-2-3s. So our job here is to deal only with the EP-SPS and
BP-FPS of each 1-2-3. This is important.

@@b1485 p
Two terms:

@@b1486 ul
<li><p>When price moves back to the BP-FPS, we call it a
<strong>TEST</strong>.</p></li>
<li><p>When price moves to (and beyond) the EP-SPS, we call it an
<strong>Excess</strong>.</p></li>

@@b1487 h3
Fitting the market with
boxes

@@b1488 p
Every 1-2-3 can be framed. But I want to frame it in a way that
interprets the price action. I do not want to draw a thousand boxes
without trying to understand the message behind the chart.

@@b1489 p
The market is like a musical composition. A box can explain its
symbols, so that a trader can read the music. This requires skill and
science.

drawing a new box that shows the sellers' power.

The Tipping Point

@@b1498 p
Next I add a TP to the box. When the next box cannot move below the
50% point, it suggests Propagation of the 1-2-3 upward.

@@b1501 p
The black line represents the 50% TP. Holding above it shows a
stronger tendency to complete the direction of Box 1.

at the boundary. So there is no Setup inside the box, only outside

@@b1528 h2
The Goodman Box with Time
(2021)

@@b1529 p
The time element reveals a great deal about the next direction. Time
shows whether buyers move fast or slow, how long they take to reach
their maximum, and when they are losing power. So time tells us when the
buyers are at the end of their movement. In this study I add time to the
Goodman Box and discuss it with charts.

@@b1530 h3
What we measure

@@b1531 p
With time in the Goodman Box, we deal only with the distance between
two or three EP-SPS, between two or three BP-FPS, and with the breakout
points.

@@b1533 p
Figure: Δ EP, the time buyers take to travel from one EP-SPS to the
next.

@@b1534 p
Δ EP shows the time the buyers take to arrive at the second
EP-SPS:

@@b1535 ul
<li><p><strong>Small delta:</strong> the buyers are very fast.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Large delta:</strong> the buyers take a long time, so
their power is decreasing.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Normal delta</strong> (compared with the other deltas):
no alarm.</p></li>

@@b1536 p
That is all we need to study time relationships. The market can only
move up or down, but time can show which of the two is stronger. Time
shows how the buyers lose their power, bit by bit.

@@b1537 p
In this study I will not look at the inverse relationship, the time
from BP-FPS to EP-SPS. I leave that advantage aside, because it deals
with box angles.

@@b1538 h3
A first example: boxes A, B and
C

@@b1540 p
Figure: The chart before the boxes are drawn: a fall, a fast rally, a
slower second fall back toward the low, then a strong rise. Real data:
USDCHF, 1-hour, 21–24 Feb 2022. (The original chart was USDCHF daily;
this one shows the same structure on the same symbol, in the window the
data covers.)

@@b1541 p
Let us draw the Goodman Boxes and try to interpret them.

@@b1543 p
Figure: Boxes A, B and C, with the new speed of Box B and Δ BP, the
time between the bottoms of A and C. Here Box B runs slightly above the
top of Box A, and Box C stops 18 pips above the bottom of Box A. Real
data: USDCHF, 1-hour, 21–24 Feb 2022. (The original chart was USDCHF
daily; this one shows the same structure on the same symbol, in the
window the data covers.)

@@b1544 p
Box A creates an Ending Point. Then Box B shows a great new speed
from the buyers, reaching a point very close to the top of Box A. This
alarms us. Buyers are fast and sellers are fast too, so we simply wait
to see which one becomes slower.

@@b1545 p
Box C arrives at the same Ending Point as Box A, but its delta is
relatively slow. The gains of Box C show that buyers are stronger than
sellers. This can be a 6&amp;6 box.

@@b1546 p
To summarize:

@@b1547 blockquote
<p>1. The buyers moved above the TP of Box A with new speed. That says
the buyers are stronger.</p>
<p>2. Box C then moved slowly and reversed inside the box several times.
That shows the sellers losing their power.</p>

@@b1548 p
This type of 6&amp;6 box can also solve a 6&amp;6 flat, by using
time.

@@b1549 p
If you are still not convinced by this example, there is much more.
When we study time, we need more data to reach the best confidence in a
trade. Do not force a trade. If you still have no confidence, follow
your heart, not the trade, especially with time.
