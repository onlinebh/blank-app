##CTX Part: Rung 5 · Acting: How Do I Manage the Trade? | Section: Trading the Line (2021)

@@b2517 h3
There Is No Future, Only Now

@@b2518 p
Every chart has a past and a current price. Time never stops, so the
current price soon becomes the past. Even the happiest moment on a chart
becomes past.

@@b2519 p
What about the future? There is no future. The future is only a
sequence that starts from the current moment. What is important is NOW,
the current moment.

@@b2520 blockquote
<p>Run fast or do not run at all.</p>

@@b2521 p
I write it like this:

@@b2522 p
<strong>Future = Current Moment + Current Moment + … + Current
Moment</strong>

@@b2523 p
Our ability is limited to the current moment. No one owns his past;
it is already gone. No one owns his future; it is only a sum. One owns
only his current moment.

@@b2524 p
When I let the current moment slip out of my control, I blame the
future. But there is no future, only the sum. The future is just a
compound of many past current moments. That is why a grandfather or a
father can anticipate his child so easily. Most of the time he is
right.

@@b2526 p
Figure: Only the current moment: the past is gone, and the future is
only the sum of the moments you manage.

@@b2527 h3
A Protected Plan

@@b2528 p
That is why we need a protected plan, a kind of economic plan. Think
of the film <em>Cast Away</em> with Tom Hanks. He could eat everything
in one day, but he knows there is no guaranteed future. So he splits
things: enough for today, the rest for the next day.

@@b2529 blockquote
<p>"Don't put all your eggs in one basket."</p>

@@b2530 p
This is a logical way to manage the current moment.

@@b2531 p
Every market moment is unique. We never get a duplicate current
moment. It is like a passport number: it cannot be duplicated, it must
be a unique ID.

@@b2532 p
We do not need to know the future of a chart to make a profit. We
only need to know the current movement and manage it.

@@b2533 h3
Driving on a Foggy Day

@@b2534 p
The market is exactly like driving on a very foggy day. We can see
only about 100 metres ahead. That is how markets look.

@@b2535 p
Anyone who drives in such conditions must be very careful and
cautious. Accidents can happen at any time. Trading the Line is like
driving carefully on a foggy day. It is risky, but with good management
one can reach the goal.

@@b2536 p
We manage the Line by concentrating and staying alert to anything
that happens to it. When we drive a Line, we do not want an accident
that causes damage. There is no right or wrong in such conditions; it is
a dangerous case, and we must protect ourselves.

@@b2537 p
Our goal is to avoid damage. When we cannot, we make the damage as
small as possible: a very soft accident.

@@b2538 p
Mr. Goodman does not accept any huge accident. Trading without a
stop-loss can lead to a large loss. Cut it quickly. Run fast.

@@b2539 blockquote
<p>"If you break even, you live to see another day." – Michael Archer,
my mentor</p>

@@b2540 p
More control on a foggy day is better than arriving quickly with huge
damage.

@@b2541 p
Anyone who wants to support a family by treating trading as a
business should not care about the destination. Care about the moment,
and drive slowly.

@@b2542 h3
The Tool That Keeps Me
Focused

@@b2543 p
One tool keeps me very concentrated when I trade TTL: watching the
Line.

@@b2544 blockquote
<p>"Follow the Lines and the Tests/Exceeds."</p>

@@b2545 p
It simply follows the BP and EP of each leg. When I operate TTL, my
goal is to save my position as much as I can, or at least to exit with
small damage. I do not like accidents. I take advantage of stop orders
to manage the trade.

@@b2546 p
The Goodman rule is clear: each time the market makes a new 1-2-3 in
the direction of the trade, and assuming you have not been stopped out,
bring your stop-loss to the most current SS EP. In short: with every new
propagation, raise the stop.

@@b2548 p
Figure: The Propagation Jumbo: each new propagation is larger, and
the stop (STP) is raised under each one.

@@b2550 p
Figure: The TTL stop routine: with each new 1-2-3 in the direction of
the trade, move the stop to the latest SS EP.

Real Driving: One Trade,
Step by Step

@@b2552 h4
1. The breakaway

@@b2553 p
Here the market breaks away with increasing volume, and the Line
starts. The operation begins after a Dagger, at the first
propagation.

@@b2555 p

@@b2556 h4
2. The first stop raise

@@b2557 p
When the market breaks the EP of the first propagation, I raise the
stop to the same level as the buy order. We are not yet at break-even,
but at least we are protected.

@@b2559 p
Figure: The buy triggers above 1 of the first propagation, and the
stop (STP) moves up to its point 2. Real data: AUDUSD, 1-hour, 14–18
February 2022. (The original chart was EURUSD 4-hour, 2021; this one
shows the same structure.)

@@b2560 h4
3. Watching the next 1-2-3

@@b2561 p
The next step is to watch the next 1-2-3, using Lines with their Test
and Exceed levels. Here there are two Lines, green and dark purple. When
the small dark-purple propagation finishes, I move my stop-loss.

@@b2563 p
Figure: Two Lines, the first (green) and the second (purple), with

@@b2564 h4
4. The time-hash

@@b2565 p
Through the time-hash our trade still makes good profit, so we
continue the drive. The green propagation grows larger.

@@b2567 p
Figure: The time hash: price works sideways between the Exceed and
Test lines, and the stop is raised beneath it. Real data: AUDUSD,

2021; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b2568 h4
5. A new count for each
propagation

@@b2569 p
I suggest making a new count for every new propagation. It helps
measure the energy behind every PTS and makes us more conservative about
the next target. When the market breaks away for the PTS 2 target, it
moves sideways.

@@b2571 p
Figure: PTS1 and PTS2 counts; a new count for each propagation helps
