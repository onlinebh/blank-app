10-4-1

@@b905 p
In Goodman trading, success means spotting 4 out of 10, with at least
1 line. That one line can sometimes cover all 6 losses. This is a very
important TTL: 10-4-1.

@@b906 h3
Many trends at once

@@b907 p
Many trends can conflict with each other. The 1-hour chart may trend
differently from the weekly chart, or from the 5-minute chart. This is
why the market is so difficult. It is only up and down, but with many,
many up and down moves running at the same time.

@@b908 p
A friend once called me. He was long EURUSD and asked if I was long
too. I told him I was short. We both made a good profit. The difference
was that I ran a station point on the 15-minute chart, and he traded the
daily chart.

@@b909 p
That is why it is a very bad idea to take market advice from anyone.
They may tell you where to get in, but can they tell you where to exit,
where to take profit, or when to raise your stop? It is better to start
with one timeframe and stay with it until the current trend stops.
Patience.

@@b910 h3
Cycles within cycles

@@b911 p
Things can become more complicated: cycles within cycles. Think of
the Earth's seasons around the Sun, and the Moon's cycle around the
Earth. Dealing with cycles helps us work out which season we are in
(what do I see now?). From that cycle we can observe the next season
(what do I want to see next?). Sometimes we also look at other cycles
related to the same chart.

@@b912 p
A chart is the same: we read it horizontally, through time and
cycles. Some currencies move with others in the same cycle, the general
economic cycle. No one in the world can govern the market and fight the
economic cycle. We can also compare cycles with each other, as
scientists do with the Earth's climate over thousands of years.

@@b913 h3
The rules of the cycle are
few

@@b914 blockquote
<p>1. For the current uptrend to continue, it must propagate a
1-2-3.</p>
<p>2. For the current downtrend to continue, it must propagate a
1-2-3.</p>

@@b915 p
I treat a complete 1-2-3 as a complete cycle. To measure the strength
of the cycle, I use a <strong>Crosspoint</strong>: a line drawn from the
BP of the FPS to the EP of the SPS. When the cycle completes, this line
crosses the SS and shows a full cycle.

@@b917 p
Figure: A complete 1-2-3 as a complete cycle: four batteries of
potential energy, joined by the Crosspoint line from BP-FPS to
EP-SPS.

@@b918 p
How does this 1-2-3 relate to "the market can only go up or down"?
There is no difference. Each rectangle is a battery of potential energy
that can launch a move. The red rectangle holds only 3 bars, which is
why the move stopped at the Tipping Point.

@@b919 p
The market only moves up and down. But to make a trend, one wave of
passion must have less energy and the other greater energy. Look at the
SS: it has a smaller battery, less "hash". That is why we work with the
1-2-3. Up and down without structure is not directional movement.

@@b932 h3
Using the Crosspoint to
find EP-SPS

@@b933 p
Instead of drawing the Crosspoint from BP-FPS to EP-SPS, we can use
it to find the EP-SPS in advance. Draw a line from BP-FPS through the
middle of the SS and extend it. You will see that it finds the EP-SPS
almost exactly. It works better with a 3-C.

@@b935 p
Figure: A Crosspoint line from point 0 through the middle of the SS

@@b936 p
The Crosspoint is also a very good tool to show the velocity of the
projectile launched from a battery.

@@b937 h3
Angle Propagation

@@b938 p
The Crosspoint is connected to the BP-FPS, so let us look first at
the angle from the BP-FPS. This angle is just a slope in two dimensions,
price and time, where x is time and y is price: y = mx + b, where m is
the slope and b the y-intercept.

@@b939 p
When the market recovers from the SS, the SPS tries to move faster
than the SS: price accelerates in much less time. All buyers now want to
move faster than in the FPS. Why? It is the Dagger concept: elastic
energy. That is why the cycle works.

@@b940 h3
Elastic energy: a tug of war

@@b941 p
When the market holds potential energy, it is elastic potential
energy, like a spring. It comes from impression force on two sides,
buyers and sellers, inside a very small range. This creates a war, with
both sides seeking maximum profit: a Nash equilibrium.

@@b942 p
The reaction in the SS is not a reaction without real effort. It is
decisive. Buyers from the FPS act like a magnetic field that tries to
stop the whole SS through impression force.

@@b943 p
Impression forces come from many origins: emotions, pressure, pain.
An impression is "an idea, feeling, or opinion about something or
someone, especially one formed without conscious thought or on the basis
of little evidence."

@@b944 p
In Goodman, the SS at 50% means half sellers, half buyers. Buyers in
the SS are losing 50% of their old profit; sellers want 50% more profit.
That is the Dagger story. Buyers want to hold their gains, so they use
impression force and increase their orders, fighting the sellers for
control.

@@b945 p
Like a compressed spring, when impression force puts the spring under
high pressure, it releases very quickly. One side must win the war, and
the stronger the impression force, the greater the success. The cycle
continues until the impression force changes its passion, its opinion
about price.

@@b946 p
We must go with the winner, the fresh flag. It is a pity, but that is
the market. It is a game, a Monopoly game.

@@b947 h3
Tick volume as impression
force

@@b948 p
The amount of trading reveals how strong the impression force is in
the SS. In forex there is no central volume, only tick volume. A tick is
a market's smallest possible price movement. For example, if a market
moves in increments of 0.25, a change from 450.00 to 451.00 is four
ticks, or one point. "The tick volume study simply counts the number of
ticks during the interval specified."

@@b949 p
Tick volume differs between brokers, but I checked several and find
about 70% of it agrees. When the mass of traders presses on a price,
that pressure counts as ticks. The mass is the mass at every broker. So
when price changes a lot, I ask why. It shows a tug of war, an
impression force pushing up or down. Measuring ticks helps show how
strong that force is.

@@b950 blockquote
<p>1. <strong>Many ticks</strong> mean a new impression force: new
traders with great emotion. When a salesman promises a crowd a deal they
believe in, many rush to buy, and the rush carries the price.</p>
<p>2. <strong>Few ticks</strong> mean no great war: the impression force
is low. Ask a child, "Do you want chocolate or carrots?" With no passion
for carrots, we know the real passion is chocolate. That is how the mass
plays. In the end, they are just human.</p>

@@b951 p
Any new impression force, especially when the market is in the SS,
confirms the elastic energy in the market.

@@b953 p
Figure: The SS comes back to the TP, where new traders bring a new
impression force and vote for the uptrend. Real data: GBPJPY, 15-minute,
24–25 February 2022. (The original chart was Bitcoin, timeframe and date
not shown; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b954 p
New traders vote for the uptrend: "Yes, we want the market to move
up." That is why elastic energy is proven in the market. At 50%, old
buyers seek new profit, or at least to hold their old gain. Sellers fear
the 50% station point and no longer want to fight. When that happens,
buyers take the flag and dominate.
