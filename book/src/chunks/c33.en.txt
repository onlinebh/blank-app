##CTX Part: Rung 4 · Deciding: Goodman's Tools and the Setup | Section: Volume Today: When Everyone Agrees (2025–2026)

@@b2142 h3
Our indicator:
Continuity + Consensus Extreme

@@b2143 p
The answer I built is my own TradingView indicator,
<strong>Continuity + Consensus Extreme (Time-aware)</strong>, which I
call ContExtT, or simply "our indicator". It does three things.

@@b2144 p
<strong>1. It classifies every bar by structure, not by
colour.</strong> A bar with a higher high and higher low is a bull bar.
A lower high and lower low is a bear bar. An inside bar or an outside
bar is undecided. The close is used only to break the tie on an outside
bar.

@@b2145 p
<strong>2. It counts continuity volume.</strong> While bull bars
follow each other, their volume is added into one buyer column. While
bear bars follow each other, their volume is added into one seller
column. Inside and outside bars do not break the count. Their volume
waits in a pending pool. If the same direction resumes, the pool joins
the streak. If the direction truly reverses, the pool is discarded. This
is the "cumulative volume" of my 2025 note: the move, not the clock,
defines the count.

@@b2146 p
<strong>3. It marks the consensus extreme.</strong> When a streak
ends, the indicator compares its final total with every completed streak
of the same side in the lookback window (50 bars by default). If it is a
new record, a gold dot is placed on the last bar of the streak. That dot
is the fold point: maximum one-sided commitment, immediately before the
reversal.

@@b2147 p
In short form the logic is:

@@b2148 p
for each bar:

@@b2149 p
if inside or outside: pending += volume

@@b2150 p
elif bull bar: buyers = (last == up ? buyers + pending + volume :
volume); sellers = 0; pending = 0

@@b2151 p
elif bear bar: sellers = (last == down ? sellers + pending + volume :
volume); buyers = 0; pending = 0

@@b2152 p
if seller streak just ended and its total &gt; highest seller streak
in lookback: mark SELLER CONSENSUS EXTREME (look to buy)

@@b2153 p
if buyer streak just ended and its total &gt; highest buyer streak in
lookback: mark BUYER CONSENSUS EXTREME (look to sell)

@@b2154 p
The signal is contrarian on purpose. A record seller streak does not
say "sell". It says the sellers have all sold.

@@b2156 p
Figure: The consensus-extreme logic applied to AUDUSD. Seller volume
builds into one unbroken streak on 24 February; when the streak breaks
at 13:00 it is the largest seller streak of the window (gold dot). Price
makes its low four hours later at 0.70942 and climbs about 195 pips by 1
March, where a record buyer streak (gold dot, top) marks the buyers'
turn to be exhausted. Panel computed from tick volume with the
indicator's rules. Real data: AUDUSD, H1, 21 February – 2 March
2022.

@@b2157 h3
Many feeds, one vote:
composite volume

@@b2158 p
One broker's tick volume is one broker's clients. So I added a
multi-broker composite to the indicator: several forex feeds combined
into one volume series. It works better than any single feed, because a
real extreme shows on every feed at once, while a feed's own noise does
not.

@@b2159 p
The open question is how to combine them. The <strong>mean</strong>
lets one loud or quiet feed pull the whole composite. The
<strong>median</strong> follows what most of the feeds say and ignores
the single outlier. I am still weighing the two.

@@b2160 p
This does not cancel what I wrote earlier, that adding platforms
cannot give the true market volume because one liquidity provider often
serves many brokers. That is still right. I am not trying to measure the
whole forex world. I am measuring agreement. In 2021 I found that about
70% of the ticks agree between brokers; the composite asks the same
question every hour: do the feeds agree that this is an extreme? Their
overlap is part of why they agree, and the median keeps one odd feed
from deciding the vote. The idea did not change; it grew.

@@b2161 p
The next plate is an illustration of the idea, not the indicator
itself. It applies the same thinking across pairs instead of brokers.
Each USD pair's tick volume is divided by its own five-day median, so
every feed is on the same scale. The bars are the median of the
three.

@@b2163 p
Figure: An illustration computed from the data: the tick volume of
EURUSD, GBPUSD and AUDUSD, each divided by its own five-day median
(lines), combined by the median of the three (bars). The composite peaks
at 24 February 18:00, when all three USD feeds agree (EURUSD 3.78,
GBPUSD 3.76, AUDUSD 2.36 times normal; mean 3.30, median 3.76). EURUSD
prints its low of 1.11062 in the same hour and rises 168 pips by 25

@@b2164 p
Note what the median did here. AUDUSD was the quietest of the three,
at 2.36 times normal. The mean was pulled down to 3.30 by that one feed.
The median stayed with the two feeds that agreed, at 3.76.

@@b2165 h3
Two currencies, two votes

@@b2166 p
Volume can show me the selling too, not only the buying. When I read
a pair at its two currencies, the volume is literally opposite. One side
shows rising bars. The other side shows falling bars.

@@b2167 p
To see it, I put two pictures side by side: the dollar index (DXY)
and the euro index (EXY). When EURUSD is about to turn, one index is
being bought and the other is being sold. If both are at maximum volume
at the same time, that is an excellent signal.

@@b2168 p
First, let me be clear about what makes EURUSD rise. There are three
possibilities:

@@b2169 blockquote
<p>1. <strong>Strong buying in the euro, and weak buying in the
dollar.</strong> Both are bought, but the euro is bought more.</p>
<p>2. <strong>Strong buying in the euro, and no change in the
dollar.</strong> The euro does all the work.</p>
<p>3. <strong>No change in the euro, and strong selling in the
dollar.</strong> The dollar does all the work.</p>

@@b2171 p
Figure: Three ways EURUSD rises. In each case the euro gains on the
dollar, but the force comes from a different side.

@@b2172 p
None of these is what I wait for. The strongest case is consensus.
Consensus is when I have two contradictory forces, both at their
climax.

@@b2173 p
In the dollar I see a buying climax. Everyone has bought the dollar,
so the dollar will fall. In the euro I see a selling climax. Everyone
has sold the euro, so the euro will rise. Two exhausted crowds, pulling
in opposite directions, and both of them act on the current pair.
Together they push EURUSD up.

@@b2174 p
This is the rule of this chapter, seen from both sides at once. A
climax is exhaustion. When everyone is buying the dollar, it means sell
the dollar. When everyone is selling the euro, it means buy the
euro.

@@b2176 p
Figure: The double climax. A buying climax in the dollar index
(record up-wave volume, so it will fall) against a selling climax in the
euro index (record down-wave volume, so it will rise). Both arrows meet
at EURUSD up. Index = equal-weight, median volume (not DXY weights).

@@b2177 p
There is one problem. DXY does not express the real strength of the
dollar. It is built with weights. The euro alone is more than half of
it, so DXY is mostly the euro read upside down. It cannot give me a fair
second opinion on EURUSD.

@@b2178 p
So I changed it. I build the index myself. For each currency I take
its seven pairs and count every pair equally. Each pair's volume is
divided by its own average, so a busy pair does not shout over a quiet
one. Then I combine the pairs with the <strong>median</strong>, not the
average. The euro pairs will vary: EURJPY can be wild while EURCHF
sleeps. The median keeps one noisy pair from deciding the vote. Where
the currency is the quote, as the dollar is in GBPUSD, I read that pair
upside down, because the index must use all of its pairs.

@@b2179 p
In 2026 I put this into one tool, which I call NetGate. It draws the
pair on top, and the two currency indexes below it. It cuts each index
into swings by Goodman's rule: a swing ends only after a 25% correction,
as Michael Archer teaches in <em>The Goodman 6 &amp; 6</em>. It adds up
the volume of each wave. A wave is a record when its volume is above the
last N waves. A double climax needs both currencies to print a record
wave on at least 3 of their 7 pairs, closing against the move. It is
counted inside the setup window, after the prior move. And the waves
must have ended. Only then do I wait for my trigger. NetGate never tells
me up or down. Direction belongs to the method.

@@b2180 p
Here is one real example, built the same way from the data I
have.

@@b2182 p

@@b2183 p
Look at 24 February. From 22 February 16:00 the euro index was sold
in one long wave of 54.3x, the largest since the prior move. From 23
February 15:00 the dollar index was bought in one long wave of 44.5x,
nearly three times its largest of the last eight waves. At 10:00 to
11:00 both indexes were near their heaviest hour, and EURUSD stood at
1.12451. That was the double climax. But I do not act on the climax. I
wait for the waves to end. The euro selling wave ended at 12:00. The
dollar buying wave ended at 17:00. At 18:00 EURUSD printed its low at
1.11062. By 25 February 23:00 it stood at 1.12739, 168 pips higher.

@@b2184 p
One extreme is a hint. Two contradictory climaxes is the signal.
