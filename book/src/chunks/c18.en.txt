##CTX Part: Rung 3 · Seeing: What Is a Market? | Section: The Analysis of Market Mind (2021)

@@b1131 h2
The Analysis of Market Mind
(2021)

@@b1132 p
In this paper I want to show how people change their beliefs over
time, and how we can read those changes on a chart. From the changes in
belief I build a hypothesis about the near future, and then I let the
market confirm it or reject it. The first part lays the ground: price,
volume and time, demand, mental energy and the Goodman rules. The second
part puts that ground to work on theoretical charts, on a set of
correlated pairs, on real examples, and finally on a full live study of
NZDUSD.

@@b1133 h3
Introduction: conflict and
belief

@@b1134 p
In every conflict, people naturally look for a way out. They do not
want the conflict; they want it to disappear. When it is intense they
cannot wait for it to end, even if it takes months.

@@b1135 p
When they see a high chance that the bad outcome will happen, they
treat it as definite fate. Then they start looking for a way through.
They know that fear is only the gate to the dark, the place of doubt and
hesitation.

@@b1136 p
So they try to believe there is a happier fate. There is a
possibility instead of the unwanted consequence, a chance for the events
they want. That good chance appears whenever there is a balance in
beliefs.

@@b1137 p
The human mind naturally weighs every premise. It tries to weaken
every conclusion that supports fearful beliefs, and it lets good beliefs
emerge by drawing new conclusions. The pages that follow explain how
people change their beliefs, and how I make a hypothesis about the near
future from those changes over time.

@@b1138 h3
Ideology: what a chart
carries

@@b1139 p
A chart carries only a few pieces of data:

@@b1140 ul
<li><p><strong>Price:</strong> high, low, open and close.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Time:</strong> the period within which price
travels.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Volume:</strong> the liquidity.</p></li>

@@b1141 h3
Logical ground

@@b1142 h4
Price, volume and time as
one space

@@b1143 p
Price tells us the final negotiation between buyers and sellers
within a period. Volume describes the quantity buyers took from sellers,
or sellers gave to buyers, in that same period. The two are connected as
dependent variables. Together with time they form a kind of 3D
space.

@@b1144 h4
Price shapes belief

@@b1145 p
Price affects the buyer's beliefs about the future. When the current
price is heavily discounted, sellers are reluctant to sell until price
reaches what they desire. Remember the headlines about the milk crash,
when Sardinian farmers dumped their milk in protest at prices.

@@b1146 p
This is the most exciting part of the supply and demand equation.
Buyer and seller are both dependent variables. Most of the time it works
like an auction: each side bids against the other, and the stronger side
sets the price.

@@b1147 h4
The types of demand

@@b1148 p
Demand is not one thing. The standard list [Demand, Wikipedia] gives
eight states:

@@b1149 ul
<li><p><strong>Negative demand:</strong> consumers dislike the product
and may even pay to avoid it.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Nonexistent demand:</strong> consumers may be unaware of
the product or uninterested in it.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Latent demand:</strong> consumers may share a strong need
that no existing product can satisfy.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Declining demand:</strong> consumers begin to buy the
product less often, or not at all.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Irregular demand:</strong> purchases vary by season,
month, week, day or even hour.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Full demand:</strong> consumers are adequately buying all
products put into the marketplace.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Overfull demand:</strong> more consumers want to buy than
can be satisfied.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Unwholesome demand:</strong> consumers may be attracted
to products with undesirable social consequences.</p></li>

@@b1150 h3
The Mental Energy Model

@@b1151 h4
Energy behind every change
in price

@@b1152 p
This model analyzes the energy behind every change in price within
the order system. Mental energy simply deals with the reaction of
belief. In the end, belief controls the space of action.

@@b1153 p
When belief is very strong, the reaction, the action, is just as
strong, but in a different direction. Belief lives in the direction of
the heart; action lives in the direction of the senses.

@@b1154 p
I call it <em>analysis</em> because it is very close to logical
analysis. There are propositions; we order them with logical operators;
and we reach an inference.

@@b1155 h4
Goals, choices and energy

@@b1156 p
Think of a team that wants to win the World Cup final. You can see
the players energized by the goal itself. The origin of energy is the
desire to achieve a goal. Goals act directly on choices, and choices are
the key to energy.

@@b1157 p
The Nash equilibrium (after the mathematician John Forbes Nash Jr.)
deals with which strategy, which choice, is best for a player to
maximize the payoff against an opponent:

@@b1158 blockquote
<p>"If two players Alice and Bob choose strategies A and B, (A, B) is a
Nash equilibrium if Alice has no other strategy available that does
better than A at maximizing her payoff in response to Bob choosing B,
and Bob has no other strategy available that does better than B at
maximizing his payoff in response to Alice choosing A."</p>

@@b1159 p
No risk, no reward. Here risk means the energy spent to maximize the
payoff inside the game.

@@b1160 h4
The market as a zero-sum
game

@@b1161 p
In game theory, "a zero-sum game is a mathematical representation of
a situation in which an advantage that is won by one of two sides is
lost by the other" [Cambridge Business English Dictionary]. The market
and its financial instruments are an example. Debit and credit in
accounting show this very simply:

@@b1162 blockquote
<p>"The rules governing the use of debits and credits are as
follows:</p>
<p>All accounts that normally contain a debit balance will increase in
amount when a debit (left column) is added to them, and reduced when a
credit (right column) is added to them. The types of accounts to which
this rule applies are expenses, assets, and dividends.</p>
<p>All accounts that normally contain a credit balance will increase in
amount when a credit (right column) is added to them, and reduced when a
debit (left column) is added to them. The types of accounts to which
this rule applies are liabilities, revenues, and equity.</p>
<p>The total amount of debits must equal the total amount of credits in
a transaction. Otherwise, an accounting transaction is said to be
unbalanced, and will not be accepted by the accounting software."
[Debits and credits definition, accountingtools.com]</p>

@@b1163 h4
Impression force

@@b1164 p
Whenever buyers believe there is a chance, an opportunity, to reach
the maximum payoff by choosing a strategy, the goal energizes their mind
and belief. The reaction becomes more intense.

@@b1165 p
Over time that energy leaves an impression. An impression is one type
of memory. It can support energy and encourage it to reach the goal.
Energy and impression are independent variables.

@@b1166 p
The crowd at a football match supports the player: that is the
impression force, an independent variable. The player energizes himself
by the goal: that is mental energy. When the impression force is at its
maximum, it becomes like a gravity force that acts as an energy
field.

@@b1168 p
Figure: The mental energy model: goal, choice, mental energy and
action, with the impression force supporting from outside.

@@b1169 h3
The Goodman rules behind the
model

@@b1170 h4
Goodman Measured Move Rule

@@b1171 p
Every swing moves by percentage from 0 to 100, and the reaction
returns from 100 towards 0. This is the total progress of each buyer's
and seller's energy. When buyers seek their maximum payoff, it is
relative to the previous payoff, and that progress shows very clearly
how buyers move.

@@b1172 h4
Propagation

@@b1173 p
Buyers and sellers always seek a maximum, and each maximum is
relative to the old one. They grow whenever they seek a new maximum.

@@b1174 h4
Goodman Setup

@@b1175 p
Whenever the market shows a 1-2-3, the move propagates into a 1. When
price returns to around the TP of that 1, or between the EP and the SS,
it sets up the next swing in the direction of the 1-2-3. Every trade
must be qualified, and it requires at least one Propagation.

@@b1176 h4
Intersection

@@b1177 p
When the market moves into an old maximum, there is almost always an
impression force there. It acts directly on today's traders who seek the
new maximum. This is the Intersection between the old and the current
maximum, and the Goodman Box shows it very clearly.

@@b1178 h4
Trend

@@b1179 p
Anyone who fights the trend is fated to lose; no one can beat the
market. The trend is simply the path along which Propagation can happen.
Propagation shows the progress of the movement and of the mental
energy.

@@b1180 h3
Methodology: from theory
to application

@@b1181 p
In this part I give theoretical examples that turn the ground above
into a constructive application. I start from the basics and go to the
deepest concepts.

@@b1182 h4
Drawing a swing

@@b1183 p
No one can take swings into a 1-2-3 and on to Propagation without
first drawing the swing correctly; it is very hard to continue without
this. As a rule, buyers and sellers always seek their maximum. So when
you draw a swing, follow it to its extreme and then draw a line. That
line represents a wave.

@@b1185 p

2-hour, 14 February to 4 March 2022. (The original chart's symbol and
dates are not known; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b1186 p
Where are the maximum point and the minimum point?

@@b1188 p
Figure: The first swings drawn to their extremes: FPS, SS and SPS.
Real data: AUDUSD, 2-hour, 14 February to 4 March 2022. (The original
chart's symbol and dates are not known; this one shows the same
structure.)

@@b1189 blockquote
<p>1. The first swing runs to its maximum, the EP of the FPS.</p>
<p>2. The second swing runs to its maximum, the EP of the SS.</p>
<p>3. The third swing runs to its maximum, the EP of the SPS.</p>

@@b1190 p
The market shows progress from 0 at the BP of the FPS to 100 at the
EP of the SPS. Now these three waves propagate into one wave, and we
test the next reaction.

@@b1191 h4
The 25% rule for a reaction

@@b1192 p
When a reaction tests the entire movement, anything less than 25%
cannot count as a reaction to the whole move. It may be a reaction to
one swing, but never to the entire movement as a Propagation.

@@b1194 p
Figure: The 1-2-3 propagates into a 1, and the next swing is read as
the reaction to that 1. Real data: AUDUSD, 2-hour, 14 February to 4
March 2022. (The original chart's symbol and dates are not known; this
one shows the same structure.)

@@b1195 p
Here the 1-2-3 propagates into a 1, and the next reaction can be read
as a reaction to that 1. At this point there is a Setup signal for a
long trade, but only after qualifying the trade with inside and outside
analysis. It is stronger when an Overlay combination shows at the
extremes. Even a Dagger setup must sit within the SS of the last
Propagation, perhaps a Propagation on a higher timeframe.

@@b1197 p
Figure: The buy Setup at the reaction to the propagated 1. Real data:
AUDUSD, 2-hour, 14 February to 4 March 2022. (The original chart's
symbol and dates are not known; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b1198 h4
Larger and larger cycles

@@b1199 p
Here is a larger 1-2-3; I call it a complete cycle to the upside.
Notice that on the downside there is never a complete cycle, never a
1-2-3. With Propagation you follow the trend easily, because you know
the market is simply propagating. Think in 1-2-3 with Propagation. So
let us draw it as a larger 1, and then watch the new SS.

@@b1201 p
Figure: A larger 1-2-3 completes on the upside, with a second buy at
its reaction. Real data: AUDUSD, 2-hour, 14 February to 4 March 2022.
(The original chart's symbol and dates are not known; this one shows the
same structure.)

@@b1202 p
Again a larger 1-2-3, then the next reaction. Everything in the
market fits inside the 1-2-3 and Propagation framework. So you know
everything: if the market always moves within this framework, nothing in
it is strange and nothing is new.

@@b1203 p
What follows is more logical analysis of this framework, but the
framework alone is enough. Its great advantage is that it always keeps
you with the trend, instead of thinking about reversals and always
fighting the trend.

@@b1205 p

@@b1206 p
Three notes on Propagation:

@@b1207 blockquote
<p>1. A larger Propagation requires a larger reaction SS. 25% of a
smaller Propagation is smaller than 25% of a larger one.</p>
<p>2. When the market comes out of a Jumbo, the new trend starts with a
small Propagation that grows larger and larger. Do not forget to treat
it as a Jumbo.</p>
<p>3. Always confirm the Propagation with Inward-Outward analysis.</p>

@@b1209 p

into one larger "1", and a reaction of at least 25% of the whole move is
the Setup.
