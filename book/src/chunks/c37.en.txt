Counter Propagation (2023)

@@b2371 p
Everything is a 1-2-3 — from trading to trending. In this short note
I show why it matters to read the chart through the lens of Propagation,
and I give some hints for spotting a counter Propagation before it ruins
a Setup.

A counter Propagation is like a nightmare for a Setup. Here are some
hints.

Following the Propagation
(2023)

@@b2411 p
In this paper I show how to follow the Propagation on a lower time
frame to judge a higher-time-frame Setup. I use six schematic examples
of 5-minute action inside a 1-hour SPS, then a real AUDCHF trade, and I
close with a two-step summary.

@@b2412 h3
Reading a Dead Chart

@@b2413 p
A chart by itself is only a description of a shift in motion. When we
read the history of a bar chart, we are on the past axis — it is dead
motion. To make an intelligent decision, we must interpret that history
so that it builds an intelligent conclusion, based on a reasonable and
factual process.

@@b2414 p
Anyone who knows Goodman knows that the core of all price action is
the 1-2-3. That action has many implications from which we can draw new
conclusions.

@@b2415 p
Here is an abstract 1-2-3 inside a larger 1-2-3. Which one has the
better odds of completing the 1-2-3 elements? In each example below,
there is a Goodman Setup on the 1H, and the question is the same:
<strong>what does the 5M action imply, and are the odds in favor of this
being the EP of the SPS on the 1H?</strong>

@@b2416 h3
Six Examples

@@b2417 h4
Example 1

@@b2419 p
Figure: Example 1 — choppy 5M action with no fresh lows inside the 1H
SPS.

@@b2420 p
Is this 5M action more bullish? It is very clear: we have a Goodman
Setup on the 1H, and this is most likely the EP of the SPS on the
1H.

@@b2421 h4
Example 2

@@b2423 p
Figure: Example 2 — 5M action that keeps making new lows, with the
last swing showing selling tiring.

@@b2424 p
This looks more bearish than bullish. Price can always move to a new
low without any hesitation. The only problem is the last swing: it
suggests the selling is temporarily tiring.

@@b2425 h4
Example 3

@@b2427 p
Figure: Example 3 — a clean, low-volatility down Propagation on the
5M.

@@b2428 p
This is the most bearish example: a down Propagation with clear
direction and low volatility. The odds of this being the EP of the SPS
are very low. We can only treat it as a Jumbo and wait for more bullish
development.

@@b2429 h4
Example 4

@@b2431 p
Figure: Example 4 — a breakaway low, then an up Propagation and a
last down Propagation that fails to make a new low.

@@b2432 p
Here the market has a breakaway. Sometimes this produces panic
behavior. Then the market tries to propagate up. The message of the last
down Propagation is that sellers are unable to make a new low — there is
a lack of sell orders. The odds are higher that this is the EP of the
SPS on the 1H.

@@b2433 h4
Example 5

@@b2435 p
Figure: Example 5 — buyers try to end the down move (a weak sister),
but sellers re-propagate lower.

@@b2436 p
The buyers try to propagate and end the down movement, but the
sellers re-propagate for a further down move. This usually happens when
the 1H has no 3-C cancel, or a bad Overlay combination.

@@b2437 h4
Example 6

@@b2439 p
Figure: Example 6 — a balancing Knot, with buyers and sellers
struggling to dominate the Matrix.

@@b2440 p
This is balancing — Knot behavior. Buyers and sellers struggle to
dominate the Matrix.

@@b2441 h4
What the examples teach

@@b2442 p
These examples give an idea of what happens on a live chart: the most
bullish, the most bearish, where selling is only struggling, and where
the Setup may fail and sellers re-propagate. They are based on many real
charts.

@@b2443 p
Simply watch the up Propagation inside the down movement. Then
measure its effect on buyers and sellers: how do they react?

Goodman points are very critical: "something may happen". When they
intersect with a Propagation, they guide the direction at that point. It
is most likely to explode.
