##CTX Part: Rung 4 · Deciding: Goodman's Tools and the Setup | Section: The 6&6 Under the Microscope (2021)

@@b2229 h3
Why the 6&amp;6?

@@b2230 p
The 6&amp;6 shows mass passion in a position of conflict. It is the
most destructive pattern, and it shows clearly how a shift in energy and
passion happens. By studying its two Matrices, the FMX and the BMX, we
can measure directly where the most passion and energy sit.

@@b2231 h3
Applying the Model to the
6&amp;6

@@b2232 p
Here I look at which pattern carries more passion, or energy, to the
upside. I build the picture step by step.

@@b2233 h4
Step 1: The Jumbo

@@b2235 p
Figure: Step 1 — the Jumbo down wave.

@@b2236 p
This energy field stops the negative passion, or at least reduces it
to a minimum. The reason is that the market has reached its Nash
equilibrium. The market now corrects the passion to check whether the
last Jumbo can survive and grow with a new down Propagation, or whether
it stops. That correction creates new energy with a positive passion to
bid the offers.

@@b2238 p
Figure: Step 2 — the first up wave after the Jumbo, the newest
passion to the upside.

@@b2239 p
We always look for a shift in passion. This point shows the newest
passion in the up wave. If the next wave corrects by less than 50%, the
negative passion has stopped and the positive passion is growing
stronger.

@@b2240 h4
Step 2: The Knot

@@b2241 p
The down wave is very small relative to the Jumbo. It shows there is
no passion left for the down move. This is clear deduction: there is a
shift in energy, confirmed by the Nash equilibrium of the downswing. Its
maximum target is tiny. There is no energy left to pull the market down.
In Goodman this is called a <strong>Knot</strong>.

@@b2243 p
Figure: Step 3 — the small down wave after the first up wave forms a
Knot.

@@b2244 p
When the market moves above EP1, it confirms the passion for an
uptrend. The path of least resistance is now up. The market is ruled by
good emotion and new energy.

@@b2246 p
Figure: Step 4 — a sharp break above EP1 confirms the up passion.

@@b2247 p
More pulling power produces more breakaway, which shows a stronger up
passion. Whenever we see that, the uptrend is confirmed. When the
movement is sharper, the passion is very strong — buyers are extremely
happy. The Nash equilibrium at EP3 is larger than at EP1, and it arrived
faster. That is a positive shift in passion; the passion is growing.

@@b2248 p
Why does a down move come after such new energy? Some hidden negative
passion remains from the Jumbo, and some sellers temporarily overcome
the buyers.

@@b2249 h4
Step 3: Testing the passion

@@b2250 p
When there is no breakaway and the angle is a little slower, the
passion is close to a retracement. The market tests whether the good
passion can survive, and at what price it can hold its gains. A test
means: is the passion strong enough to create a pulling force that beats
all sellers in a real struggle? If the positive passion wins, the test
is successful.

@@b2251 p
In this case the market gives a very unreliable target. When the
passion is strong, it must make a good breakout of BP1.

@@b2253 p
Figure: The rally fails to break BP1 well (orange line) — an
unreliable target.

@@b2254 p
Here I measure the speed of the impression force with a slope line
drawn through two anchor points. The line shows how fast positive
passion responds to the next correction.

@@b2255 p
We now have the FMX. This Matrix acts as a centripetal force — like a
magnetic field that attracts positive passion from all over the world.
This magnetic field cannot be destroyed. It is a major sign of a
potential shift in energy.

@@b2257 p
Figure: The slope line (green) through two anchor points measures the
speed of the positive passion.

@@b2258 p
When the market gives only a small Nash equilibrium — a very low
maximum profit — positive passion is taking profit very quickly.

@@b2259 h4
Step 4: Testing the magnetic
field

@@b2260 p
When the next negative passion reacts behind the slope line, some
impression force wants to grow the passion upward, and the market
responds faster.

@@b2262 p
Figure: A negative reaction behind the slope line, followed by a
quicker response.

@@b2263 p
But when the next response has too little energy to make a new Nash
equilibrium, there is no interest or passion to move the trend up. The
trend is over, yet the magnetic field remains. I call it <strong>hidden
positive passion</strong>; it can generate more positive passion
later.

@@b2265 p
Figure: Yellow arrows mark where the passion stops relative to
EP1.

@@b2266 p
If the passion stops above EP1, the market can hold its gains. If it
cannot move above EP1, the weakness is greater. Both are weakness, but a
rally that cannot exceed EP1 is the weaker; the energy is gone very
quickly.

@@b2267 ul
<li><p><strong>Note 1:</strong> The weaker the market now, the better it
tests the positive passion. In such a struggle we need to know that
positive passion wins, so that sellers cannot come back. Then we have a
strong shift in energy.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Note 2:</strong> When the weakness is very low, and the
positive passion still cannot continue the trend after 50%, it shows
more weakness than positive passion.</p></li>

@@b2268 h3
Big Deal Notes: Two BMX
Conditions

@@b2269 p
Let us assume two conditions:

@@b2270 blockquote
<p>1. <strong>The clearance sale.</strong> The market wants to sell all
its stock. It is like a shop full of old items at a big discount, eager
to clear them. Here the BMX is very sharp, because they want to remove
all sell orders and take profit from the FMX effect (the magnetic
field). After that, no one wants to sell any more.</p>
<p>2. <strong>The sticky price.</strong> The FMX creates a conflict in
passion. Imagine a shop that offered a big discount for a long time (the
Jumbo). In summer it raises its prices (the FMX). When summer ends, it
does not want to return to the old price quickly, because it thinks
customers accept the new one. Here the BMX forms slowly and takes a long
time.</p>

@@b2271 p
Which is better? I think the first, because they know the depression
is over and recovery (expansion) has begun.

@@b2272 h3
Testing the
Centripetal Force of the FMX

@@b2273 p
Let us test the centripetal force from the 1-2-3 FMX. The break of
BP2 is the first test of whether the uptrend can survive again. As we
can see, this sign shows negative passion. Why? In the old wave, at the
first yellow arrow, the Nash equilibrium is very low relative to the
second arrow. The newer wave shows a better Nash equilibrium — though of
course still less than the Jumbo's.

@@b2275 p
Figure: Yellow arrows compare the Nash equilibrium of the old wave
and the newer wave after the break of BP2.

@@b2276 p
If the next rally shows a good Nash equilibrium, the market can move
up again. If it shows only a very small one, expect it to move down
more.

@@b2278 p
Figure: Three arrows track a shrinking Nash equilibrium in the
rallies — buyers accept ever smaller targets.

@@b2279 p
Here the positive passion has only a small Nash equilibrium, so the
market weakens further until the weakness is satisfied and the energy is
lost. The downtrend resumes with very negative passion, and we test the
FMX magnetic field. The market wants to dump all its offers in front of
the buyers.

@@b2280 p
The third arrow shows how quickly sellers dump on the buyers, and the
buyers accept a small Nash equilibrium. The buyers are weak and the
sellers are strong. The next breakdown confirms the downtrend. Comparing
the first, second and third arrows shows the shift in passion: the
impression force is weak, the pulling power is weak, and demand is very
weak.

@@b2281 p
When that happens, shifting energy can change the trend from up to
down. To confirm it, price should break down through EP1 of the BMX.

@@b2283 p
Figure: The last down wave reaches a smaller Nash equilibrium than
the first down wave from EP1 of the BMX.

@@b2284 p
In the last down wave, the Nash equilibrium is smaller than in the
first down wave. The response of negative passion is much weaker than at
its origin at EP1 of the BMX. The negative passion is struggling against
the FMX positive passion. In other words, this is the final test: does
any negative passion want to go further? The FMX effect makes the market
look, from another perspective, like a double bottom.

The time at the left red arrow (FPS) is longer than at the right red

larger Nash equilibrium — more gain, more profit. The SPS had less gain,
less profit and less time dominating the market, so it reverses
easily.

@@b2297 p
There is also no breakaway in the BMX. All this evidence shows that
the FMX caused the shift in energy.
