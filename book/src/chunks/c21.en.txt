@@b1371 h2
Observe the Market Behavior
(2022)

@@b1372 p
In this short paper I use one chart (originally AUDUSD daily, redrawn
here on real EURUSD 2-hour data with the same structure) to show how
Goodman Wave Theory reveals the way one swing intersects with other
swings. I borrow two ideas from physics, external force and Newton's
first law, to explain why a swing changes speed or direction exactly
where it meets an earlier structure.

@@b1374 p
Figure: A down 1-2-3 (FPS, SS, SPS) whose SS stalls near the TP of
the earlier swing, and whose SPS ends in a box past the end of line AA,
the 3C. Real data: EURUSD, 2-hour, 21 February to 4 March 2022. (The
original chart was AUDUSD daily, April to October 2022; this one shows
the same structure.)

@@b1375 h3
Why did the SS fail
to attract more buyers?

@@b1376 blockquote
<p>"Experiments have thoroughly verified that any change in velocity
(speed or direction) must be caused by an external force. The idea of
generally applicable or universal laws is important not only here—it is
a basic feature of all laws of physics."</p>

@@b1377 p
The same applies in the market. An external force is the cause, and
its effect is a change in speed or direction.

@@b1378 p
On this chart it is clear. The SS failed to attract buyers when it
intersected with the TP. The TP acted as a Probability Cause. It
produced the effect of an external selling force that changed the
velocity of the SS.

@@b1379 h3
How far will the SPS run?

@@b1380 blockquote
<p>"A body at rest remains at rest, or, if in motion, remains in motion
at a constant velocity unless acted on by a net external force." —
Newton's First Law</p>

@@b1381 p
Selling will keep dominating price until it intersects with a
Probability Cause, such as the end of line AA, where the 3C principle
comes into play. Watch the box: the price bars start closing at the top.
That change in direction reflects the external force coming from the
Probability Cause, the 3C. The price structure that follows will be
complex or flat.

@@b1383 p
Figure: The Probability Cause as an external force: a swing keeps its
velocity until it meets a TP or the 3C line, then changes speed or
direction.

@@b1384 h3
The Goodman trader's habit

@@b1385 p
The Goodman trader always watches these Probability Cause points.
They are an important step in observing market behavior, and in making
an inductive inference about what comes next.

@@b1386 h2
Using
GWT as an Identification Tool for Intermarket Analysis (2025)

@@b1387 p
This study looks for relationships between currency markets. A topic
like this can easily lead to analysis paralysis, where you end up
nowhere. So I begin with a clear definition of trend, and then I use GWT
to identify how each market stands against that definition.

@@b1388 h3
A working definition of
trend

@@b1389 p
There are many definitions of trend. The one I find closest to the
reality of the market is this:

@@b1390 blockquote
<p><strong>A price trend remains dominated by the Propagation until it
is cancelled by another Strong Intersection.</strong></p>

@@b1391 p
This definition holds an inverse relationship between two forces: the
Propagation and the Intersection. The Intersection plays the key role in
halting the Propagation.

@@b1393 p
Figure: The working definition of trend read top-down, with the
Propagation running until a Strong Intersection halts or cancels it.

@@b1394 h4
A first example
(first read on USDMXN, 2-hour)

@@b1396 p
Figure: The EP-SPS meets the EP-FPS area, and the Intersection
cancels Matrix 2 in favour of Matrix 1. Real data: USDCAD, 30-minute,
23–25 February 2022. (The original chart was USDMXN 2-hour, 20 Dec 2024
– 10 Jan 2025; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b1397 p
On my original USDMXN 2-hour chart (shown here on real USDCAD data),
the EP of the SPS met the EP-FPS, and it was also close to the EP-SS.
This cancelled Matrix 2, which had been the last dominant matrix. Its
dominance ended when it intersected with the components of Matrix 1.

@@b1398 p
The market then resolved in the direction of Matrix 1. The
Intersection was strong enough to stop Matrix 2 from dominating the
trend.

@@b1399 h3
The top-down approach on
USDMXN

@@b1400 p
In GWT we analyze at least three matrices: Outward, Watch and Inward.
Each one shows its own matrices and its own Goodman points.

On the weekly chart (redrawn here on 4-hour data), the current price
is close to the Over-3C count, which represents an Equilibrium.
Meanwhile, the Propagation is still making new EPs, and it may develop
into a Line. Let us zoom in to the daily.

@@b1405 h4
Daily (Watch)

@@b1407 p
Figure: A trending market turns into a trading range. Real data:

@@b1409 h4
2-hour (Inward)

@@b1410 p
Zooming in to the 2-hour chart brings us back to the first example
above. Matrix 2 was cancelled, and price resolved in the direction of

@@b1411 p
All of this follows the definition: a price trend remains dominated
by the Propagation until it is cancelled by another Strong
Intersection.

@@b1413 h3
Why this matters for
intermarket analysis

@@b1414 p
A study like this opens many doors for exploring the relationships
between markets. Descartes' philosophy does not let me accept any
easy-going correlation between currencies. At the same time, I need to
identify the big players that dominate the currency market.

@@b1415 p
Then I try to explore the correlation approximately, as our
definition of trend reveals it. So I applied the same reading to EURUSD,
GBPUSD and USDCAD. The charts under each pair redraw its weekly and
daily structure on real 2022 data, often from another pair; each caption
names the data shown.
