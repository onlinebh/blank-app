@@b1898 h2
A
Short Study in Overlay Combination and the Concept of Bounces
(2022)

@@b1899 p
In this short study I introduce the idea of bounces and show how
balancing a combination of propagations can sharpen our view of what
buyers and sellers are able to do at an Intersection.

@@b1900 h3
What is a bounce?

@@b1901 p
By studying charts for a long time, you realise that every movement
in the market can be divided into bounces. A bounce is simply a price
level that generates momentum, either down or up. Price can only go up
or down.

@@b1902 ul
<li><p>A bounce that generates momentum up is a <strong>bull
bounce</strong>.</p></li>
<li><p>A bounce that generates momentum down is a <strong>bear
bounce</strong>.</p></li>

@@b1903 p
This concept is very similar to the triple spread.

@@b1905 p
Figure: Bull bounces along a rising line, where each EP of
propagation is higher than the last.

@@b1906 p
If a bounce generates a higher EP of propagation compared with the
last EP, it means a bull propagation. Conversely, a lower EP means a
bear propagation.

@@b1907 h3
Balancing a combination

@@b1908 p
The chart is a combination of different propagations: bear
propagations, bull propagations, timehash or Knot.

@@b1910 p
Figure: When the second propagation makes a lower EP, it is only a
reaction, and the next lower high reacts to it.

@@b1911 p
In cases like this the bounces are weak, both bull and bear. They are
mostly in equilibrium: a tug of war where no one succeeds.

@@b1912 p
By balancing these propagations, their bounces and their EPs, we see
whether the move is bull, bear or just a Knot. This enhances our vision
of the ability of buyers and sellers. Will they generate a bull bounce
that breaks the Intersection, or just move away from it?

@@b1913 h3
A visionary idea: the wood
wall

@@b1914 p
Think of the Matrix Overlay, in outward analysis, as a wall of MDF
wood.

@@b1916 p
Figure: Bull propagations pushing toward the Matrix Overlay wall,
then turning into a bear propagation as buyers fail to hold the
price.

@@b1917 p
Is this combination of propagations most likely to break the MDF
wood? Or is the combination too weak to break it? The change from bull
propagation to bear propagation shows weakness: buyers are simply unable
to hold the price.

@@b1926 h2
Matrix Overlay with Volume
(2021)

@@b1927 p
In this paper I show how volume, read through Goodman theory, can
confirm a Matrix Overlay. I treat volume as energy, divide the box into
Goodman levels, and watch where energy is spent and whether price
transits to the next level.

@@b1928 h3
The Matrix Overlay in brief

@@b1929 p
A Matrix Overlay is when buyers or sellers return to the original
point after a substantial move. It is stronger when the return to the
original point comes by 1-2-3 movements. When those 1-2-3 movements are
cancelled by a 3-C, it gives more confidence to enter the trade. Of
course, we trade in the direction of the Matrix Overlay.

@@b1930 p
The 3-C is evidence that the move against the MO trend has no energy.
Volume can help confirm that the counter-trend move has no energy, or
that the MO trend has more energy than the counter-trend. It is relative
energy.

@@b1931 p
<strong>Caution:</strong> without a proper construction of the MO and
the 1-2-3, volume will mislead you about the true energy.

@@b1932 p
Here are several examples. Each one explains an important
concept.

@@b1933 h3
Example 1: EURUSD, 1-hour

@@b1935 p
Figure: Price returns to the origin point in the buyers' zone after a
1-2-3 up; the last down move is cancelled by a 3-C. Real data: EURUSD,
1-hour, 22 Feb – 1 Mar 2022. (The original chart was EURUSD 1-hour, 6–11
October; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b1936 p
On EURUSD the price returns to the origin point, in the buyers' zone.
This MO has two 1-2-3s and a cancel in the last 1-2-3. It looks exactly
like a 6&amp;6 Setup. But here I want to quantify the MO. On its own,
the volume shows nothing but columns with no specific meaning. That is
why interpreting volume with Goodman theory is so important.

@@b1938 p
Figure: The same chart with the MO propagated into boxes. Real data:
EURUSD, 1-hour, 22 Feb – 1 Mar 2022. (The original chart was EURUSD
1-hour, 6–11 October; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b1939 p
Let us propagate within boxes and split the boxes into levels to
track the energy. I divide each box into Goodman points as horizontal
levels, then watch the volume in that frame. It looks hard, but it is
only at the start, to show how volume works. Later we do not need the
levels; they are just for visual purposes.

@@b1940 p
I draw a horizontal line for each Goodman point in the 1-2-3. Our
focus is the first box.

@@b1942 p
Figure: The first box with a horizontal level drawn at each Goodman
point and the energy (volume) at each. Real data: EURUSD, 1-hour, 22 Feb
– 1 Mar 2022. (The original chart was EURUSD 1-hour, 6–11 October; this
one shows the same structure.)

@@b1943 h3
Energy levels

@@b1944 p
The first box reminded me of energy levels in an atom.

@@b1945 blockquote
<p>"Atomic electron transition is a change (or jump) of an electron from
one energy level to another within an atom or artificial atom. It
appears discontinuous as the electron "jumps" from one energy level to
another, typically in a few nanoseconds or less. It is also known as an
electronic (de-)excitation or atomic transition or quantum jump."
(Wikipedia)</p>

@@b1947 p
Figure: An electron absorbs energy to jump levels; price spends
volume to transit between Goodman levels.

@@b1949 p
Figure: A close-up of the first box with its four levels and the
volume beneath. Real data: EURUSD, 15-minute, 24–25 Feb 2022. (The
original chart was EURUSD 1-hour, 6–11 October; this one shows the same
structure.)

@@b1950 p
Energy can be measured by E = hv; on the chart, E = Volume.

@@b1951 p
<strong>Concept 1:</strong> what the volume shows is the quantity of
energy spent (absorption) at every level.

@@b1952 h3
Calories: the closest
example to volume

@@b1953 p
Let us discuss the behaviour of energy through its closest example to
volume: calories.

@@b1954 ul
<li><p>The input for calories is food.</p></li>
<li><p>The output for calories is body consumption, or activities
(sports and so on).</p></li>

@@b1955 p
In the same way:

@@b1956 ul
<li><p>The input for volume is the transactions of buyers and sellers at
a known moment.</p></li>
<li><p>The output for volume is activity: the absorption forces that
transit price to the next level.</p></li>

@@b1957 p
A Harvard study lists the calories burned in 30-minute
activities.

@@b1959 p
Figure: Calories and volume compared as input and output of energy,
with the Harvard figures for 30-minute activities.

@@b1960 p
Volume, like calories, is relative energy: the energy spent from one
activity (absorption) level to the next. So let us study the energy at
each activity level.

@@b1961 h3
Reading the first box
level by level

@@b1963 p
Figure: The first box with four numbered levels and the volume at the
first arrow. Real data: EURUSD, 15-minute, 24–25 Feb 2022. (The original
chart was EURUSD 1-hour, 6–11 October; this one shows the same
structure.)

@@b1964 p
We have 4 levels of activity, and the volume shows the energy spent
at each one. At the first arrow, energy increased at level 1. This is
the beginning of the MO, the BP. That energy shows sufficient buyers at
level 1. To confirm it, buyers must make price absorb the next visual
level, 2.

@@b1966 p
Figure: A successful transition to level 2 on less volume. Real data:
EURUSD, 15-minute, 24–25 Feb 2022. (The original chart was EURUSD
1-hour, 6–11 October; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b1967 p
The energy (volume) is less at level 2, but with a successful
transition and absorption.

@@b1969 p
Figure: A small sideways box where volume rises but fails to reach
level 3. Real data: EURUSD, 15-minute, 24–25 Feb 2022. (The original
chart was EURUSD 1-hour, 6–11 October; this one shows the same
structure.)

@@b1970 p
Then the market moves sideways in a small box. Energy and price are
in some conflict, with no clear sign. Volume increased at the last arrow
but failed to transit to the next level, 3.

@@b1972 p
Figure: Price leaves the small box as buyers raise the energy toward
level 3. Real data: EURUSD, 15-minute, 24–25 Feb 2022. (The original
chart was EURUSD 1-hour, 6–11 October; this one shows the same
structure.)

@@b1973 p
At the last arrow, price moved out of the smaller box as buyers
increased the energy to level 3.

@@b1975 p
Figure: The red arrow marks the buyers' failure to move above level
3. Real data: EURUSD, 15-minute, 24–25 Feb 2022. (The original chart was
EURUSD 1-hour, 6–11 October; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b1976 p
The red arrow shows the buyers' failure to move to the next level,
higher than 3. When that happens, look at the price and the arrow: this
energy will return price to level 2. If the chart stopped here, the
price action would suggest a Nest setup.

@@b1977 h4
The Nest

@@b1979 p
Figure: The Nest setup: a 1-2-3 down on rising energy (red) and a
last rally on falling energy (yellow). Real data: EURUSD, 15-minute,
24–25 Feb 2022. (The original chart was EURUSD 1-hour, 6–11 October;
this one shows the same structure.)

@@b1980 p
Here the chart shows the Nest setup. First a 1-2-3 down with
increased energy. At the yellow arrow, energy decreases. This behaviour
suggests buyers are scrimpy. A 1-2-3 down on increased energy, and a
last rally on decreased energy? The tendency is for sellers: lower low,
lower high.

@@b1981 p
How is it a 1-2-3? Its SS has broken the BP of the FPS. The aim of
any 1-2-3 is to show progress, the ability to move, when the smaller
1-2-3 propagates into a larger 1-2-3. The larger 1-2-3 proves the
smaller one. As the red SS shows, the downside propagates into a larger
1-2-3.

@@b1983 p
Figure: Red, yellow and green arrows: rising, falling and rising
energy that fails to push below level 2. Real data: EURUSD, 15-minute,
24–25 Feb 2022. (The original chart was EURUSD 1-hour, 6–11 October;
this one shows the same structure.)

@@b1984 p
After the increased volume at the red arrow, the yellow shows
decreased energy, and the green arrow shows an increase to transit below
level 2. Instead of moving lower, the chart gives a nested setup.

@@b1986 p
Figure: At the green arrow, volume rises but cannot transit to the
lower level. Real data: EURUSD, 15-minute, 24–25 Feb 2022. (The original
chart was EURUSD 1-hour, 6–11 October; this one shows the same
structure.)

@@b1987 p
Just enter on the lower time frame, before the bar closes. The level
by itself shows nothing. What matters is the new behaviour at the green
arrow: volume increased but could not transit to the lower level.

@@b1989 p
Figure: Black arrows: energy lifts price above level 3, then hugs it
as a Dagger forms toward level 4. Real data: EURUSD, 15-minute, 24–25
Feb 2022. (The original chart was EURUSD 1-hour, 6–11 October; this one
shows the same structure.)

@@b1990 p
At the first black arrow, energy increased and price transited above
level 3. At the second black arrow, energy decreased but hugged level 3,
and price moved into a Dagger and broke out close to level 4.

@@b1992 p
Figure: The full first box with all the energy arrows from level 1 to
level 4. Real data: EURUSD, 15-minute, 24–25 Feb 2022. (The original
chart was EURUSD 1-hour, 6–11 October; this one shows the same
structure.)

@@b1993 p
From this it should be obvious that volume shows the energy behind
these levels.
