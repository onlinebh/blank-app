The Screenshot Track
Record (2025-06-17)

@@b3604 p
Some years ago a wealthy man who wanted to start an FX fund asked
Michael for advice. One day he announced that he had found a great AI
system. Michael asked for the developer's track record so he could
analyze it. "All I get is screenshot of pretty MT4 chart, lots and lots
of colors, numbers, line... I tell man doesn't make sense - MQL4 not
strong enough script to make real AI." The man invested anyway and lost
nearly all of it in two days.

@@b3605 p
<em>The lesson:</em> a screenshot is not a track record. Ask for the
statement, the trade list and the drawdown; if all you get is pretty
colors, you already have your answer.

@@b3606 h3
The Bargain Chess Books
(2024-11-04)

@@b3607 p
Michael once ordered three very large boxes of chess books from a
dealer in the Czech Republic, mostly foreign-language editions. The
books cost about $200 and the postage almost as much, "but still good
deal." Then customs called: they wanted $1,100 to release the boxes. He
found a customs agent who negotiated it down to $300, but her own fee
was another $300. "Think I get great bargain on books, cost me
$1000!!!"

@@b3608 p
<em>The lesson:</em> count every cost before you call it a bargain.
Commissions, spreads and swaps do to a "cheap" trade what customs did to
the cheap books.

The Saxophone (2023-06-26,
2023-06-29)

@@b3610 p
In college a friend from the music department begged Michael to
switch his major to music theory and composition; he said Michael had
perfect pitch and a gift for melody. Michael stayed with philosophy, and
with the drum. Fifty-five years later, in June 2023, he bought an alto
saxophone. "First time spend money on myself in long time!!" And true to
his own method: "Maybe I take a few private lessons to get good start,
like chess, ahaa!"

@@b3611 p
<em>The lesson:</em> it is never too late to begin, but it is always
too late to unlearn a bad start. At seventy-three Michael still took his
own advice: get a teacher first, play later.

@@b3612 h3
The Glass Ball at
Heidelberg (2021-10-16)

@@b3613 p
Not every Archer story is about Archer. Some are stories he collected
and passed on because of what they teach. This one, he said, "actually
happened at the University of Heidelberg, sometime in the early
1960s."

@@b3614 p
A physics professor leaned on a large decorative glass ball outside
the physics building and burned his arm. Strangely, the shady side was
hot and the sunny side was cool. He spent hours at his blackboard and at
last produced a page of equations proving why the shady side should be
the hot one. The next day he proudly showed his graduate student. They
walked outside together, just in time to see two workmen in asbestos
gloves turning the ball 180 degrees. "At this time of day the way the
sun shine makes one-half of the ball extremely hot; people have burnt
themselves. So we always rotate the ball."

@@b3615 p
<em>The lesson:</em> before you explain the chart, check what
actually happened on it. A beautiful theory can prove anything, even the
opposite of the truth. Charlie said the same thing in his own way: look
at the market first.

@@b3616 p
When I read these stories together, I hear the same voice Michael
heard from Charlie in that car on the mountain pass: a teacher who
prefers an example to a lecture. He gave me the stories; I am giving
them to my students, as he hoped I would.

@@b3617 h1
Rung 10 · The
System: How Does It All Move Together?

@@b3618 p
Nine rungs took the trader apart. Being asked who stands behind the
chart. Knowing asked how truth is learned. Seeing asked what a market
is. Deciding asked whether this is a Setup. Acting asked how to manage
the trade. Measuring asked how much to risk. Doing Right, Feeling and
Learning asked how we treat others, where we trade from, and who carries
the method on.

@@b3619 p
This last rung puts the pieces back together. A trader is not a list
of skills. He is a system: every part pushes on every other part, and
the pushes come back. In August 2026 I wrote to Michael that I was
"thinking on system dynamic with A Philosophical Look at System
Dynamics." This chapter is what that thinking became.

@@b3620 h3
The parts that move

@@b3621 p
A system is a set of parts with arrows between them. In my trading
the parts are the rungs. What I see (Seeing) feeds what I decide
(Deciding). What I decide becomes an order (Acting). The order becomes a
number in my journal (Measuring). The number touches my feelings
(Feeling). My feelings either wait for the next good setup or chase the
market. Then the review (Learning) changes how I will see the next
chart. The loop closes.

@@b3622 p
I saw this loop early, before I had a name for it. On 2021-05-10 I
wrote after one of Michael's lessons:

@@b3623 blockquote
<p>"Qualify the trades, Proactive, FeedBack Loop. Three simple secrets.
to investigate before investing and after."</p>

@@b3624 p
On 2022-12-09, writing about chess, I tried to say it again:

@@b3625 blockquote
<p>"I think its means the loop between visualize and tactics as a
learning process. In every win or lose. This loop function must
work."</p>

@@b3626 p
And on 2022-11-09:

@@b3627 blockquote
<p>"Trading success is based on the compound factor. Loops."</p>

@@b3628 p
A loop can work for you or against you. System dynamics gives the two
kinds names: reinforcing and balancing.

@@b3629 h3
The reinforcing loop

@@b3630 p
A reinforcing loop feeds itself. In the plate below every arrow on
the left carries a plus sign: more of one thing gives more of the next.
A run of wins brings confidence. Confidence turns to overconfidence.
Overconfidence makes the lot bigger and the rules looser. A bigger lot
gives bigger wins for a while, and bigger wins bring more
confidence.

@@b3631 p
Nothing inside this loop says stop. That is its danger. It grows
until something outside it breaks it, and in trading that something is
one large loss.

@@b3632 p
Navid's story in Rung 9 is this loop exactly. He did FANTASTIC on the
first thirty trades with a small lot and only the Under. Then he doubled
the lot for the second thirty; Michael sensed he was in a hurry to make
real money. After about ten trades he said, "I'm going for it!" The loop
had no brake. When Michael and I discussed the story on 2023-05-18, I
wrote that traders who lose their edge "enter into the psychological
cycle. Between extremely optimistic to extremely pessimistic." The same
loop, at the scale of a whole market, made my real-estate example in
that letter: land nobody wanted in the 1970s, and everyone still buying
in 2023 at the extreme of optimism.

@@b3634 p
Figure: The trader as a dynamic system. Left: the reinforcing loop
(R), every link positive. Right: the balancing loop (B), one negative
link that turns it back. Below: the tipping point where one loop gives
way to the other.

@@b3635 h3
The balancing loop

@@b3636 p
A balancing loop corrects itself. It has one link that points the
other way, a minus sign, so the loop pulls the system back toward a goal
instead of running away.

@@b3637 p
My balancing loop is built from the rules of Rung 6. Losing trades
bring drawdown. Drawdown makes me cut the lot, because the lot is never
free: I decide my maximum drawdown first and divide it into thirty
trades. On 2025-03-05 I put it this way:

@@b3638 blockquote
<p>"Keep in mind we are trading the money not the market... you do not
have many souls in the market, have only 30 souls!"</p>

@@b3639 p
A smaller lot means I survive and get back to breakeven, which was
Charlie's first goal for every trader. Survival gives me time to learn:
I label every trade, I watch the Golden Ratio every ten trades, I do the
bathtub review. Learning lowers the next drawdown. That is the minus
sign that closes the loop.

@@b3640 p
On 2023-11-24 I wrote the rule in one line:

@@b3641 blockquote
<p>"If not happy with that dropdown , i just lower the risk per trade .
Self-control very important in these drop down."</p>

@@b3642 p
And on 2022-02-11 I wrote what happens when the loop is missing:

@@b3643 blockquote
<p>"the first alarm after 30 trades one losing ... when the door it's
closed by now beliefs after 30 trades of losing. the other door will
open by the feedback process."</p>

@@b3644 p
The balancing loop does not stop losses. Nothing does. On 2025-01-04
I wrote, "So its kind of balance rather than trying to eliminate risk
from the equation." The loop only keeps a loss from becoming a
catastrophe.
