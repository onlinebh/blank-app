@@b666 h2
The Problem of
Imperfect Induction (2022)

@@b667 p
Imperfect induction is the kind of reasoning we use every day on the
chart. We see a cause work a few times and we expect it to work again.
In this short paper I want to show the problems it faces, as they appear
to me.

@@b668 h3
Problem 1: no proof of a
definite cause

@@b669 p
The aim of induction is to establish a general law: every natural
phenomenon has a cause. But without proof of a definite cause, it is
probable that some other cause is at work. It is even probable that the
phenomenon happens without any cause.

@@b670 p
When we consider these possibilities, it follows that a cause does
not necessarily produce its effect.

@@b672 p
Figure: Old highs that looked like a definite cause for sellers to
stop the rise; each one failed, and price closed above them. Real data:
AUDJPY, 1-hour, 28 February – 4 March 2022. (The original chart was
AUDJPY, 1-hour, 4–21 July 2022; this one shows the same structure on the
same symbol, in the window the data covers.)

@@b673 h3
Problem 2: will the
cause work in the future?

@@b674 p
The purpose of induction is to generalize the cause so that it works
in the future. It claims that every time the cause happens, it will
produce the phenomenon. So we must prove that the cause will keep
working in the future.

@@b676 p
Figure: A high that stopped buyers this time; a cause observed in the
past does not promise the same effect in the future. Real data: AUDJPY,
1-hour, 21–24 February 2022. (The original chart was AUDJPY, 1-hour, 30
August – 16 September 2022; this one shows the same structure on the
same symbol, in the window the data covers.)

@@b678 p
Figure: The two gaps of imperfect induction: an unproven cause, and
an unproven future.

@@b679 h3
What this means for the
trader

@@b680 p
Every time one makes an imperfect induction, one will face these
problems. One must realize this, above all when something important
rests on it.

@@b681 h2
Propagation and Probability
(2022)

@@b682 p
In this AUDCAD study, I want to show how the Propagation lens makes
the chart clearer and easier to grasp. I also want to show how we can
add probability causes, one by one, until we can make an inductive
inference about a reversal.

@@b683 h3
The Jumbo and the first
positive cause

@@b685 p
Figure: The Jumbo: YY, the X-Propagation from EP-Jumbo, and the
LS-Propagation back to the same low at A (here A holds 0.6 pip above the
EP-Jumbo low). Real data: AUDCAD, 1-hour, 23 February – 2 March 2022.
(The original chart was AUDCAD, 4-hour, 15 June – 24 August 2022; this
one shows the same structure on the same symbol, in the window the data
covers.)

@@b686 p
The Jumbo is a flat decline. It is a propagation, a concentration of
selling force that comes from seeking profit in a downtrend. The selling
at YY is the fastest swing in the Jumbo.

@@b687 p
An external purchasing force enters the market at EP-Jumbo. It tries
to concentrate its buying power by X-propagating the swings, from a
1-2-3 to a larger 1-2-3. The buying force appears able to concentrate,
taking all the shares until it meets selling at YY.

@@b688 p
This gives <strong>probability positive cause #1</strong>. It
suggests that the new external force from the X-propagation may change
the velocity (speed and direction). When the next selling swing comes
with contraction, that confirms it, and we add the cause. A positive
cause raises the chance that the trend velocity changes from down to
up.

@@b689 p
Note that the LS (last downswing) Propagation comes with contraction
rather than expansion.

The advanced
tools and the second positive cause

@@b691 p
If we add some advanced tools, the Triple Spread and the Boxes, the

@@b693 p

@@b694 p
Watch how the selling force contracts against the external force, the

@@b695 blockquote
<p>1. Box 1 represents the Jumbo. Here the selling force could hold for

<p>2. Box 2 is very clear: the force is smaller in distance and in

<p>3. Box 3: finally, the sellers are unable to concentrate and

@@b696 p
This adds <strong>probability positive cause #2</strong>.

@@b697 h3
Volume and the third
positive cause

@@b698 p
Also watch what happens when price moves to EP-Jumbo, which is a
probability cause point. The external force sets in, and buying appears
at A. Volume rises to a new level at A, which shows buyers are present.
This is reflected in a bar closing near its high: a reversal bar.

@@b699 p
This adds <strong>probability positive cause #3</strong>.

@@b700 h3
The inductive inference

@@b702 p
Figure: The 3C read at point 2, and the cancel: on 2 March buyers
close above points 1 and 2, a higher high that cancels the sell setup,
as in the original. Real data: AUDCAD, 1-hour, 23 February – 2 March
2022. (The original chart was AUDCAD, 4-hour, 15 June – 24 August 2022;
this one shows the same structure on the same symbol, in the window the
data covers.)

@@b703 p
By considering causes #1, #2 and #3 together, one can make an
inductive inference that a reversal will follow. At the least, these
causes will change the speed and direction. This is confirmed at Box 3,
where price moves away from the Triple Spread Line. There is no selling
spread anymore!

@@b704 p
The trend is now in the hands of the buying force. It will continue,
as in Newton's first law, until an external force sets in at a
probability cause point, the 3C. Then price changes direction.

@@b706 p
Figure: Three probability positive causes feeding one inductive
inference: a change in speed and direction.

@@b707 p
If one zooms into Box 2, one will see the same behavior as a nested
Overlay, and one can practice the theory there.

@@b710 p

@@b711 p
I also want you to notice the gap and the price reversal as a

propagation that comes from the 15-minute chart at EP-Jumbo. Does anyone
wonder where the XX bar comes from? It is simply a concentration of
buying force, coming from a different level. Notice the buying present
at different levels of volume; both reach a new level.

@@b712 h3
A word of caution

@@b713 p
This study reveals a high chance of a reversal. But that is not
always the case. One must be flexible and ready for more complex
situations, when the chart shows a probability negative cause and a
positive cause at the same time.

The maker of every maker

@@b726 p
