@@b358 h3
How I Came to Goodman

@@b359 p
The first time I saw markets, I was struck by how the market moves in
a phenomenal structure. The chart always has upswings and downswings. At
the same time, it generates many smaller swings on a 1-minute chart.
When I look at higher time frames, like the monthly, I see many swings
too.

@@b360 p
Then I asked: how are these waves generated by buy and sell orders? I
was trying to understand the noumenon behind them.

@@b361 p
I started with a basic assumption. The secrets of these waves — how
they occur, why they occur, and the type of each swing — would be the
source code of the market.

@@b362 p
As in any scientific field, we have more questions than real answers.
The more I dug, the more I found myself lost in the forest. What if it
is just a kind of chaos, random, without any real explanation behind
it?

@@b363 p
I did much research, trying to catch any clue that reveals the
market's secrets in a logical and predictive way. I found a book,
<em>The Spirals of Growth and Decay</em> by Daniel T. Ferrera, one of
the most expensive books I have seen. At the end of the book he writes:
"Interesting article I recently found on Swing wave and the 50% rule".
It was "Goodman's Swing Count System — by M. Duane Archer".

@@b364 p
At that moment I felt I was finally in the right place.

@@b372 h3
Why Popular
Strategies Fail in Transition

@@b373 p
Everyone is a genius in a bull market, especially in High DM / Low V.
But let us count the popular strategies that fail when there is a
transition.

@@b374 h4
1. High DM / Low V

@@b375 p
Strategies that fail: mean reversion (RSI, Bollinger reversion),
counter-trend trading, stochastic reversals, grid trading.

@@b376 p
The dominant psychology here is greed: people want to enter the
market as soon as possible. The result is very low volatility. The
failing strategies hope for a return to the average, but it never comes,
because the buying is aggressive.

@@b377 h4
2. High DM / High V

@@b378 p
Strategies that fail: pullback trading, tight stop-loss trend
systems, moving-average reversion, fixed-size breakouts.

@@b379 p
High volatility has a great effect on many strategies. A trader with
a tight stop finds himself out of the market while the market moves
higher without him. Violent volatility creates fear. Trend-following
strategies underestimate volatility and take losses without profit.
Unfortunately, the pullback strategy cannot deal with high volatility
and produces wrong signals.

@@b380 h4
3. Low DM / Low V

@@b381 p
Strategies that fail: trend following (EMA cross, SuperTrend),
Donchian breakouts, momentum trading, MACD trend systems.

@@b382 p
Never short a dull market. Traders gain false confidence because
volatility is low and the market seems so predictable. There are many
small breakouts but no follow-through. It is a trap.

@@b383 h4
4. Low DM / High V

@@b384 p
Strategies that fail: all trend-following systems, breakouts (mostly
false), deep mean reversion (RSI below 20 or above 80), channel and
regression trading.

@@b385 p
With low direction, traders do not know which direction to trust.
Overbought and oversold signals stop working. Traders see a "pattern"
that does not do what they expect. Trend followers lose in this play:
there is no trend, and fear creates high volatility.

@@b386 blockquote
<p>"Systems fail because they were developed and optimised over ME
skewed data sets, NOT because of any intrinsic deficiency in
back-testing."</p>

@@b387 p
I see many traders make a nice profit for some months, then lose what
they gained, and they do not know why the strategy stopped working. The
market is more complex than traders imagine.

@@b388 blockquote
<p>"There is a degree of indeterminacy and/or 'noise' in the market,
making them much less than 100 percent predictable."</p>

@@b389 h3
Matrices within
Matrices: the Propagation lens

@@b390 blockquote
<p>"No matter how thin you slice it, there will always be two sides." —
Spinoza</p>

@@b391 p
It is whole worlds inside whole worlds: Matrices within Matrices,
each at the same time part of larger Matrices. This is the Propagation
lens. Without it, we are walking on a dark and cloudy day; the lens is
the light in the dark. We cannot see what is going on in the market
unless we connect the Matrices in the best fit.

@@b392 p
For example, a Setup is a Propagation with a breakaway from the
smaller Matrix that connects to the larger Matrix. This means a Setup is
a market-environment transition, far away from a system curve-fitted to
one ME cluster.

@@b393 p
It works relatively well across all ME clusters. It does not depend
on the current ME; it anticipates and exploits the transition between
environments.

transition.

@@b400 p
Multi-timeframe ≠ Propagation lens.
