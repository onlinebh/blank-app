@@b1832 h3
Part 2: How to
construct a logical combination

@@b1833 p
Instead of using guesses, expectations or tips, a logical combination
can set up and promise a very good return. But first: what do we mean by
trading?

@@b1834 h4
Trading as an engineering
balance

@@b1835 p
In engineering, a balance is kept on a conserved quantity (money,
energy, total mass) in a system. The system can be a single process
unit, like buying and selling a stock, or an entire process, like a
campaign:

@@b1836 p
Input + Generation − Output − Consumption = Accumulation

@@b1837 ul
<li><p><strong>Input:</strong> what enters through the system
boundaries.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Generation:</strong> what is produced within the
system.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Output:</strong> what leaves through the system
boundaries.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Consumption:</strong> what is consumed within the
system.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Accumulation:</strong> what builds up within the
system.</p></li>

@@b1838 p
For example, every 3 months a trader puts 10,000 USD into trading
(input). He loses 2,000 USD (consumption), wins 8,000 USD (generation),
and takes 5,000 USD home (output). So 10,000 + 8,000 − 5,000 − 2,000 =
11,000 USD accumulated after 3 months.

@@b1839 p
The real risk in trading is when the market cannot generate any
profit (generation), or consumes a lot of your money (consumption). Both
happen within the system, the process unit. So trading is just a
machine: a trading vehicle. In engineering, anything lost within the
system is called consumption. When a vehicle consumes more money than it
generates, it must be fixed.

@@b1840 p
From this, trading is 100% safe when you choose the best vehicle, one
that generates more than it consumes. When you start from a logical
basis, you easily know what you want. The Goodman process helps you
understand which vehicle suits you. The Overlay combination must sit
within the system, to make sure it can generate more than it
consumes.

@@b1841 h4
Four logical combinations
of extremes

@@b1842 p
I found 4 logical combinations that show the most extreme points,
where Y (generation) &gt; X (consumption). When they occur, they give a
buy or sell signal:

@@b1843 blockquote
<p>1. <strong>Sell signal:</strong> extreme happiness on the daily chart
and extreme happiness on the 1-hour chart.</p>

@@b1844 ul

MO or PO.</p></li>
<li><p>1-hour: 6&amp;6, Goldilocks, Dagger.</p></li>

@@b1845 blockquote
<p>2. <strong>Buy signal:</strong> extreme sadness on the daily and
extreme sadness on the 1-hour. "When there's blood in the streets, you

@@b1846 ul
<li><p>Daily: 6&amp;6, Goldilocks, Dagger; better at an MO or

<li><p>1-hour: 6&amp;6, Goldilocks, Dagger.</p></li>

@@b1847 blockquote

@@b1853 p
My job is to qualify the Overlay. When the market is not in one of
these cases, there is no signal. I trade only extremes, as a type of
Nested Overlay, with the MO and PO supporting the Nested Overlay.
