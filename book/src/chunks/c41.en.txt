Under, Over and Line

@@b2664 p
Every trade I take ends in one of a few places. It goes to BE. It
reaches the Under. It reaches the Line. Or it goes Over and I am out at
a loss. Later I started to label every trade this way, and I will tell
that story in the next chapter.

@@b2665 p
For management, the rule is plain. Protect at BE. Take part of the
trade at the Under. Let the rest run to the Line. If I close everything
early, my average win shrinks and my whole system weakens. If I never
take anything, the market takes it back. The Under pays the bills; the
Line pays for the years.

Line.

@@b2668 h3
The order of qualification

@@b2669 p
In July 2024 you sent me a sequence for deciding on a trade. I kept
the steps, but I changed the order to match how my own mind works. I
called it my "Top -Down approach":

@@b2670 p
"1)Do Outward analysis (Propagation Chart) if nothing interesting
look for another chart." (July 3, 2024)

@@b2671 p
Then I identify a basic 6 &amp; 6 or Goldilocks setup. Then the
Parameter analysis on a clean chart. Then Inward analysis. Then I decide
if the setup is qualified enough for me. Then I consider the Risk-Reward
Ledger. Only then do I watch for an entry.

@@b2672 p
The first step is the important one. If the propagation chart does
not interest me, I do not force it. I move to the next chart. Most of my
protection comes from the trades I never take.

@@b2673 h3
Money-management profiles

@@b2674 p
By December 2024 I had put the whole method into a short list for
myself: setup, parameters, entry, outside analysis, and money
management. For money management I named five profiles: "Money
management : Walts ,Samba ,Bert,tiny dancer ,Fixed ." And under it I
wrote: "It's simple , more systematic . but Require Each of them
separate profiles ! . each profiles Fits as a Risk Strategy model ."
(December 25, 2024)

@@b2675 p
Each of these is a different way to split and manage a position: how
much at the Under, how much for the Line, when to move to BE, how much
to add. Fixed is the simplest: one size, no changes. The point is not
which profile is best. The point is that I choose one before I enter,
and I know which risk model I am running. I do not invent the management
in the middle of the trade.

@@b2676 h3
A trade I was not proud of

@@b2677 p
I want to include one trade where I broke my own rules, because it
taught me more than ten good ones.

@@b2678 p
In August 2024 I bought AUDCAD. I made money. But when I looked at
the numbers, I was not happy: "117p Risk : 59p Reward , which is a bad
raito . almost 1 RISK TO 0.5 Reward". The lesson I wrote that day was
short: "Entry is important , Risk /Reward . such mistakes aren't
suitable for PRO! ." (August 29, 2024)

@@b2679 p
A profit does not make a trade good. If I keep risking two to make
one, the arithmetic will catch me sooner or later, even with a high win
rate. This trade was a win on the statement and a loss in the
process.

@@b2680 h3
Questions before I accept a
risk

@@b2681 p
In February 2026 I tried to make my risk decisions more formal.
Before I accept a trade, I ask a few questions. How could this setup
fail? Which Overlays am I relying on, and which am I ignoring? What is
the impact if I am wrong? Is there whitespace, and is the chart
clean?

@@b2682 p
Then I wrote the rule for acceptance: "Logically the risk accept when
1) the impact of risk its very low impact that it's fine to live with.
2) the risk is very low compare to profit." (February 2, 2026)

@@b2683 p
The next day I added something I did not expect to write: "the
smaller risk if avoided will generate larger risk! . don't think about
profit , profits that's what we want , but if not protect the process
will lead to big loss." (February 3, 2026)

@@b2684 p
A trader who refuses every small risk will one day take a large one
out of frustration. Accepting small, known, livable risks is how the
process stays alive.

@@b2685 h3
What ties it together

@@b2686 p
Looking at these five years, I see one movement. In 2021 I only knew
that BE came first. In 2022 I learned to make the small trade pay for
the big one. In 2024 I put the qualification in my own order, named my
management profiles, and was honest about my mistakes. In 2026 I turned
risk acceptance into questions.

@@b2687 p
None of this replaced Goodman. It is the frame around Goodman. The
Measured Move tells me where the Line is. Management decides whether I
am still in the trade when price gets there.

Thirty Souls: The
Arithmetic of Survival

@@b2691 p
I have always believed that trading is a business, and that the
hardest problem in any business is cash flow. I told you this in my
early letters, Michael: I came to the market from account management,
and managing money is the part I enjoy. Over five years my rules about
size changed shape many times. The core never changed. I decide what I
can lose first, and only then do I look at the chart.

@@b2692 h3
2021: the strict plan

@@b2693 p
In February 2021 I sent you my plan. It was short and strict:

@@b2694 p
"1) I never risk more than 1.5 for every trade . or i am gamble 😂 2)
I never open trades more than 6% of all my balance . 3) I never increase
my lot until I double my balance" (February 22, 2021)

@@b2695 p
There was more. After 10 trades, if the result was not right, I
reduced my lot. I never entered without a risk/reward. And if a trade
was good but too expensive for me, I let it go and waited for the next
one. I wrote then that I did not care about single trades: what matters
is who wins at the end of the game.

@@b2696 p
Reading it now, I see a young rule set. It was about limits. It did
not yet ask where the limits come from.

@@b2697 h3
2022: the Green and Red signals

@@b2698 p
In October 2022 I tried to connect size to psychology. A trader can
be conservative, aggressive, or urgent, and each needs a different
allocation. Above, there is a greedy zone; below, a danger zone. I did
not want to move between them by feeling, so I made a rule:

@@b2699 p
"Green, Red signal policy.each critical points 5% lose 10% profit."
(October 22, 2022)

@@b2700 p
A green signal, after 10% profit, allows the size to grow: for
example, 1% of 100,000 plus 1% of the last 10% profit. A red signal,
after a 5% loss, does the opposite. It forbids the losses from
expanding, and the size comes down.

@@b2702 p
Figure: The Green / Red signal policy. Size changes only at critical
points.

@@b2703 h3
2023: divide the drawdown by
thirty

@@b2704 p
By April 2023 the rule had turned around. I no longer started from a
percentage per trade. I started from the drawdown I was willing to live
with:

@@b2705 p
"I have always decided, on my max DD, then I divide it into 30
trades. if my capital is 20,000 . and I want only to risk 5%DD . the max
loss is 1000, I divide it into 30 trades opportunity . so the max risk
for every trade is 33 USD." (April 13, 2023)

@@b2706 p
Then every 10 trades I review and study the Overlay combinations. One
to five trades a week. That was the whole plan.

@@b2707 p
This was a real change. In 2021 the per-trade risk decided my
drawdown. In 2023 the drawdown decided my per-trade risk.

@@b2708 h3
2023: the Golden Ratio and the
labels

@@b2709 p
In August 2023 I began labelling every trade: BE, Under, Over, Line,
and for the losses, mistake, bad entry, or lack of discipline. "Now each
of the trades from 300 has a specific label. These labels for sure
changes the game." (August 11, 2023) Once a trade has a name, a losing
streak is no longer a fog. I can see whether it came from the market or
from me.

@@b2710 p
In October 2023 I called the control of risk the chassis of a system,
like the chassis of a race car, and I put the Golden Ratio inside it:
the percentage of winners in each 10 trades, and the average win against
the average loss. "Golden Raito is standing behind the Probability of
Ruin." (October 6, 2023) By December I was calling it the "Blood heart"
(December 12, 2023).

@@b2711 p
In May 2024 I wrote down how to read it: "If my winning trades less
than 4 out of 10. Its suggest ether have issue in one of psychology
pattren. Or my choice in Market environment dosnt fit well." And if the
average win falls below 1:2, "I know i make very bad judgment. I did not
take under nor going for lines." (May 25, 2024)

@@b2712 p
So the ratio is not only a score. It is a diagnosis. It tells me
where to look.
