@@b1438 p
There is a positive correlation between EURUSD and GBPUSD. Both tend
to propagate lower, and both have an Intersection that may halt the
Propagation. USDCAD and USDMXN both propagate higher, and they also have
Intersections that may halt the Propagation: the EP-SPS on USDCAD, and
the trading range that USDMXN is still in.

@@b1439 p
The more Intersections we see across the group (EURUSD, GBPUSD and
the others), the stronger the tendency toward a reversal. Likewise, more
Intersections in the negatively correlated markets, such as USDCAD, will
stop their Propagation higher. That in turn supports a reversal in
EURUSD or GBPUSD.

@@b1440 p
Finally, by tracking all these markets together, we will see momentum
revolving between them. One market becomes the leader, and the others
become laggards.

@@b1441 h2
Markets in Relation,
Revisited (2025–2026)

@@b1442 p
In 2025 I used Goodman Wave Theory as an identification tool between
markets: a trend is Propagation in control until a strong Intersection
cancels it, and that definition let me compare EURUSD and GBPUSD against
USDCAD and USDMXN. This chapter is what came after. My view of how
markets relate has become simpler, and more careful. A relationship
between two charts is not a rule. It is one more witness.

@@b1443 h3
A witness, not a rule

@@b1444 p
I never trusted the easy correlation tables. Two pairs that moved
together last month can split apart next week. What I look for is not
the number between them but the process inside each one. On 2024-06-10 I
wrote about how a plan forms:

@@b1445 blockquote
<p>"Two kind of process meets in one directions. Or works all together
in one time . Sellers from diffrent Matrices."</p>

@@b1446 p
That is the heart of it. When sellers appear in different Matrices of
one chart, the read gets stronger. When the same kind of process appears
in a related market at the same time, it gets stronger again. The other
market does not tell me what to do. It confirms, or it warns.

@@b1447 h3
Looking at the other pairs

@@b1448 p
Most days I do not watch one chart. I watch a small family. On
2025-07-18 I described what happens when one chart changes my mind:

@@b1449 blockquote
<p>"Ideas change by overlay deduction. Sometime i see a re propagation.
But look other pairs oh this is a nice intersections and count."</p>

@@b1450 p
One pair shows a re-Propagation, so the trend looks alive. But in the
sister pairs I find Intersections and a 3-C count. That does not cancel
my chart. It makes me patient. If the family is turning, the pair that
still propagates is often the last to turn, not the exception.

@@b1451 p
My watch notes from 2026 read like this. On 2026-02-02: "here i watch
cadchf / usdcad MO." On 2026-09-08, a longer list:

@@b1452 blockquote
<p>"NZDCAD is the same as EURCAD, but I prefer EURCAD. … GBPAUD is the
best so far. It has an outward overlay."</p>

@@b1453 p
Two pairs can tell the same story. When they do, I do not trade both.
I choose the cleaner one, the one with the Outward overlay or the better
whitespace. Sister charts are for choosing, not for doubling.

@@b1454 h3
One leads, another lags

@@b1455 p
On 2026-06-05 I wrote, almost in passing, that things move "like
currency . one leads while another lag". This is what I mean, and the
plate below shows it with real data.

@@b1457 p
Figure: EURUSD, GBPUSD and AUDUSD on 1-hour closes, each as % change
from 14 Feb 2022. They share one dollar and move together hour to hour,
yet EUR was already the weakest before the 24 Feb shock and finished
three weeks down 3.8%, while AUD finished up 3.3%. (Real data: 1-minute
bars resampled to 1 hour, 14 Feb – 4 Mar 2022.)

@@b1458 p
Look at what the data says. Hour by hour, the three pairs are
strongly related: the correlation of their hourly moves is between 0.68
and 0.76. Anyone who stops there will say they are "the same trade". But
over three weeks they went in different directions. From 16 Feb, EURUSD
stayed under the zero line while GBPUSD and AUDUSD rose. By 23 Feb, EUR
was down 0.5%, the pound was down 0.1% and the Aussie was up 1.3%. EUR
was the weakest before the shock day, and after it, EUR led the fall.
GBP followed more slowly. AUD dipped with the others for one day, then
went its own way.

@@b1459 p
So a high correlation does not mean the same direction. It means the
same dollar. The difference between the pairs is the difference between
the other currencies, and that is where the information is. The weak one
leads when the dollar is bought; the strong one resists. When I look for
a short, I want the leader. When I look for the end of a move, I watch
the lagger: if the lagger finally makes its Intersection, the family is
turning.

@@b1460 h3
Consensus across the family

@@b1461 p
The 2025 study ended with a rule I still use: the more Intersections
across the group, the stronger the tendency toward reversal. Since then
I add a second test. Before I accept a market story, I ask the chart to
approve it. On 2025-10-10, watching gold, I wrote:

@@b1462 blockquote
<p>"This also must approve it by the chart. A nice setup in 1 hour will
approve this assumption."</p>

@@b1463 p
News, fear, a strong dollar, a weak euro: these are only assumptions.
The relationship becomes useful when it shows up as structure, a 1-2-3,
a Propagation, an Intersection, a count. If the story is true, the setup
appears. If no setup appears, I keep the story to myself.

@@b1464 h3
Relation as a money question

@@b1465 p
The newest idea is that relationships between markets belong to money
management, not only to analysis. On 2025-08-30 I wrote:

@@b1466 blockquote
<p>"Flat jumbo + setup . Is less risky than a propagation jumbo with a
setup."</p>

@@b1467 p
and a few lines later:

@@b1468 blockquote
<p>"Each setup may play a rule . Maybe for a more gains . Or a hedge ,
or a decrease the risk."</p>

@@b1469 p
When I hold more than one trade, they are not separate. Two trades in
different Market Environments can balance each other. Two trades in the
same environment, on the same currency, are one trade with double
size.

@@b1470 p
That is why, in 2026, I built the danger into my trading expert as a
guard. Layer 9 of the Poka-Yoke system, which I described to Michael on
2026-09-17, is the Correlated Exposure Barrier: it "Automatically
detects if two open trades are compounding your risk on the same
currency (e.g., holding EURUSD and GBPUSD simultaneously)." Look again
at the plate. Long EURUSD and long GBPUSD in that February would not
have been two trades. They would have been one bet against the dollar,
lost twice.

@@b1471 h3
And for the record

@@b1472 p
Relationships also matter in how I measure. On 2025-01-30 I wrote
that my sample of trades must not be bent by them: "The most important
they are not correlation different time". If ten trades in my series of
thirty are really one dollar move, my Golden Ratios lie to me. The
record must hold independent samples, each with an equal chance, or it
is only a story I tell myself.
