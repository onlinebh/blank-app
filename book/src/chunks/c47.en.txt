Repetition instead of
emotion

@@b2975 p
How do you leave the cycle? Not by fighting it. In February 2022 I
wrote that habit comes from the repetition of action. In October 2024 I
said it more sharply:

@@b2976 p
"Focus on Repetition instead of emotions. Let emotions distil in the
sea of Repetition." (October 2, 2024)

@@b2977 p
Repeat the same process with consistency, with a very low standard
deviation, and the emotions settle like sediment. They do not disappear.
They stop steering.

@@b2978 h3
Waiting

@@b2979 p
Charlie's sitting ducks took me a long time to understand. In May
2022 I wrote: "believe me it takes me a deep breath to understand it.
waiting is the key. keys that belong to the gates, from ignorance and
unhappiness to power!" (May 14, 2022)

@@b2980 p
Waiting is not doing nothing. It is refusing to spend one of my
thirty souls on a trade I do not want. Most of my good decisions are
trades I never opened.

@@b2981 h3
Process, not one result

@@b2982 p
In May 2024 I wrote about a Goodmanism, "Don't draw big conclusions
from little data," and what it means for a trader: "people tend to take
the source of knowledge from the "Results" of their actions . , and
forget about the right decision they make ." (May 7, 2024)

@@b2983 p
A good decision can lose. A bad one can win, as my AUDCAD trade did.
If I learn only from outcomes, I learn the wrong lessons. Even in 2021 I
wrote that it is like football: the most important thing is who wins at
the end of the game.

@@b2984 h3
The crowd

@@b2985 p
In October 2022 I told you about the clearest lesson I ever had about
other people. "I trade from 25 trades, i win 21 trades out of 25." Then
I traded together with others, "and when I traded with some I lose 10
trades in a row ." When I reviewed it, the reason was simple: "I simply
i did not trade for My needs, i just trade for begin Right, wrong."
(October 11, 2022)

@@b2986 p
With a crowd, I was no longer trading my plan. I was trading to be
right. And the crowd keeps you looking at the wrong things. You cannot
even blame them; they are a group. Charlie knew this. He kept other
people's opinions away from his facts, so he would not lose the power of
his judgment. I learned to do the same: avoid, and be alone with the
chart.

@@b2987 h3
Greed is a road to poverty

@@b2988 p
In May 2023 I compared the "crazy mode" of greedy people with my own
way: "i trade many of trades because "low risk idea" , i can controll
the risk . even if fail." The rule that governs it is one line: Profit
Exceeds Expense. (May 27, 2023)

@@b2989 p
A year later I wrote it again, more simply: "Just take a little
eveytime from the market. Let equity curve very very smooth. Slowly
rich." And I remembered Charlie's story about greed: if I am greedy, the
system will fail. (May 25, 2024)

@@b2990 p
Greed feels like wanting more. In practice it is the fastest way to
have less. It is the y = k/x of the emotions.

@@b2991 h3
Self-control in drawdowns

@@b2992 p
Drawdowns are part of every system, and my answer to them is very
plain. In November 2023 I wrote: "If not happy with that dropdown , i
just lower the risk per trade . Self-control very important in these
drop down." (November 24, 2023)

@@b2993 p
I do not argue with the drawdown. I do not try to win it back fast. I
make the next steps smaller until I am calm again.

@@b2994 h3
Mistakes are controllable

@@b2995 p
In February 2025 I gave myself a definition I still use: "Mistakes =
any controllable action in wrong time." (February 3, 2025) A loss is not
a mistake. A loss is the market. A mistake is something I could have
controlled and did not: a late entry, a moved stop, a skipped rule.

@@b2996 p
My cure is a feedback loop: capture every trade, make a bathtub
analysis, make a small enhancement, and never change the core. By April
2026 I saw it as risk language: "Making mistake is a operations risk to
the system." (April 3, 2026) The market, the system and the trader must
all be aligned. A good system with a trader who cannot follow it still
loses.

@@b2997 h3
Discipline is a fruit

@@b2998 p
People tell traders to be disciplined as if discipline were a muscle
you flex. In June 2025 I wrote what I believe: "Becuase the source of
discipline is depends how one have a high self esteem . A very high
evaluation about himself. The displane just a reaction or a fruit of
high self esteem." (June 1, 2025)

@@b2999 p
If I respect myself, I keep my promises to myself. My trading plan is
one of those promises.

@@b3000 h3
Systematic, not perfect

@@b3001 p
In February 2025 I wrote: "What happens to a trader , looks for
perfection , insted we need to think in Systematic approach , make
little changes (enhance) every time . never change the core ." (February
17, 2025)

@@b3002 p
Perfection makes a trader rebuild everything after every loss. A
system lets him improve one small thing and keep going.

@@b3003 h3
Relaxed and excited

@@b3004 p
Charlie said a trader must be relaxed and excited at the same time.
In December 2024 I copied another of his lines into a letter to you
because it said the same thing: "You have to be laser focused but at the
same time ‘loose-as-a-goose’ ." Under it I wrote about my own practice:
"I like to trade in many times around 0 ~ 5 % ROR. Laser focus. As well
calm." (December 10, 2024)

@@b3005 p
This is where I trade from. Excitement without calm becomes the crazy
mode. Calm without excitement misses the sitting duck. Fear is tense and
flat, and the real power evaporates. The place I want is the corner
where both are high.

@@b3007 p

@@b3008 h3
Looking back

@@b3009 p
In 2021 I knew the mind was programmed backwards. By 2026 I had a
map: the cycle, the cure of repetition, the patience of waiting, the
danger of the crowd and of greed, the plain tools of smaller size and

@@b3012 h2
29. Navid

@@b3013 p
<em>Excellent trader, wrong week.</em>

@@b3014 p
Michael told me this story twice, saying it was important for me.
Navid was a student around 2012: an excellent understanding of Goodman,
a quick learner who saw patterns easily, a young man, very nice and
polite. After the course he started a 30-trade exercise in October.
Every week he sent his MT4 summary. For the first thirty trades he
traded only the Under, with no Line. "He does FANTASTIC." He traded only
five thousand lots.

@@b3015 p
Then he started a second 30-trade exercise at ten thousand lots.
Michael sensed he was in a hurry to start making real money. Michael
kept warning him. After about ten trades Navid said, "I'm going for
it!"

@@b3016 p
He made two terrible mistakes. First, he raised his lot size too
fast, to one hundred thousand. Raise lot size slowly. Second, he did it
at the top of his equity cycle, after a good run of profitable trades.
Always add after a down cycle in equity. "Counterintuitive, but must
do!" The pressure was too much. He made four or five bad trades, and
everything from October was gone. He wrote to say thank you for
everything and goodbye, and went back to his old job. "I cry for him
when I read it," Michael wrote.

@@b3017 p
<strong>What it means.</strong> The method held. The trader was
rushed. Size is where an honest trader gets hurt.

@@b3018 p
<strong>Try this.</strong> Write a size-increase rule with a number.
For example: increase only after a drawdown, and by no more than one
step.

30. The man who looked
for the magic bullet

@@b3020 p
<em>Charlie was right.</em>

@@b3021 p
In January 2025 Michael told me about a man in Holland who had bought
the two Kindle Goodman books. Michael answered his questions, sent him
charts, and tried to help. But the man was obsessed with finding a magic
bullet in indicators instead of Goodman. Michael went back and forth
with him in four or five emails with charts, explaining why indicators
cannot work in the long run. No luck: the man was sure that if he
tortured the numbers enough he would find something nobody had found in
millions of hours of research.

@@b3022 p
Michael put the emails together into a post on his site, but he saw
what Charlie saw: for most people, no hope. "I could have just listened
to Charlie, but I had to learn for myself. AHAHAHA!"

@@b3023 p
When he saw a screenshot of the simulated-trading platform the man
was using, Michael could only imagine Charlie's answer: "Just more rope
to hang yourself, Dad. The markets can only go up or down."

@@b3024 p
Months later, he spent twenty minutes explaining to a new trader why
moving averages and oscillators do not work in the long term: ME, DM, V,
curve-fitting. "I could go in the corner and talk to the wall and the
wall would understand better."

@@b3025 p
<strong>What it means.</strong> Some lessons only arrive when the
student is ready, and no explanation will hurry them. Share what you
know, and then let the market teach the rest.

@@b3026 p
<strong>Try this.</strong> Remove one indicator from your chart.
Trade a week without it and write down what you notice.
