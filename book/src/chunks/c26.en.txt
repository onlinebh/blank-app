Overlay Combination (2021)

@@b1686 p
In this study I want to show exactly how an Overlay combination
affects two things: risk and reward. A strong combination may promise a
safe trade; a weak one increases the risk. In the second part I build
the combination on a logical basis and show four combinations of
extremes that I trade.

@@b1687 h3
Too many choices

@@b1688 p
In any market in the world there is at least one product or service
to attract buyers. But with so many products, so much innovation and
technology, the consumer faces many types of one item. There are lots of
competitors.

@@b1689 p
When you want to buy a phone, you face Apple, Samsung, Huawei and
others. Even within one company there are many models and sizes. I read
some new sales books saying the best strategy is to give the customer
only three choices.

@@b1690 p
Today's stock market is no different. I find software that searches
for patterns in more than 30,000 stocks around the world. That is just
more rope for traders to hang themselves.

@@b1691 p
It is easy to get emotional about a stock and rush to buy something
you know nothing about. This behaviour must be avoided. What looks like
an opportunity is not always a real opportunity.

@@b1692 p
In Goodman theory we start from the general and move to the specific.
Very few people want to look at things from a deep angle. The real
opportunity is in the details, deeper and more specific.

@@b1693 h3
The equation of every market

@@b1694 p
Every market has an equation, simple mathematics. I buy any amount of
something at X, then I sell it at Y:

@@b1695 blockquote
<p>1. Y &lt; X: I paid more than I sold for. I am a loser.</p>
<p>2. Y = X: a waste of time, but I can try again.</p>
<p>3. Y &gt; X: I am a winner.</p>

@@b1696 p
There is no fourth case in any transaction. We are all obedient to
this equation.

@@b1698 p
Figure: The three outcomes of every trade and the basic requirement
of Y &gt; 2X.

@@b1699 p
If you keep taking losses, you will soon damage yourself. That is why
the market is so dangerous. Goodman put the Overlay there to help us
avoid losing trades, not always, but most of the time. You can stay in
the market for a long time by making at least breakeven or enough
profit.

@@b1700 h4
Two words to avoid

@@b1701 p
Two ideas must be avoided when we deal with the market:

@@b1702 blockquote
<p>1. <strong>Always.</strong> There is always someone smarter than you
who wants to take your money.</p>
<p>2. <strong>Guarantee.</strong> There is no guarantee the market will
follow your words.</p>

@@b1703 p
The market lives on another planet. The market is the BIG BOSS. Facts
sometimes become strange, but in the end they are facts.

@@b1704 p
There is an advantage too. Once you start, you will never find peace
in stopping, because you know there is only a small distance to your
next dream. And there is extra news: "Compound interest is the eighth
wonder of the world. He who understands it earns it … he who doesn't …
pays it." (Albert Einstein)

@@b1705 h4
Who loses?

@@b1706 p
Again, simple logic. At the end of any deal, the future shows who is
the loser and who is the winner:

@@b1707 ul
<li><p>Losers are those who buy and the price moves down.</p></li>
<li><p>Losers are those who sell and the price moves up.</p></li>

@@b1708 p
In both cases Y &lt; X. Successful trading means buying something
that at least promises you can sell it higher. Otherwise you are the
loser.

@@b1709 p
There is risk in every minute in the market. "No Risk No Reward"
(Goodman). The risk is buying a stock you cannot sell higher, where Y
&lt; X. That is why we want to know which trade can promise at least Y =
X. But the excellent trade is when we sell at more than twice, and
above. Better still is trading the Line.

@@b1710 p
So we must always look at:

@@b1711 blockquote
<p>1. The risk of the trade: X.</p>
<p>2. The potential profit: Y &gt; X.</p>
<p>3. At least Y &gt; 2X.</p>

@@b1712 p
Take a trade only when there is a real potential of Y &gt; 2X. That
is the basic requirement for every trade.

@@b1713 h3
Why the combination matters

@@b1714 p
An Overlay combination with a Goodman Setup helps us find that
requirement. Every combination with any Setup directly affects risk and
reward. A bad combination may hurt both. Trading with not enough
Overlay, or with a weak Overlay, causes greater risk.

@@b1715 p
From this point I must give a caution. You need judgement and
experience, which come from trading for months and years, until
judgement becomes a sixth sense. Then you can become an excellent
picker.

@@b1716 h4
The logic behind a
combination

@@b1717 ul
<li><p><strong>Strongly bullish combination:</strong> buyers can pull
the market up. The trend is dominated by happy passion and controlled by
greed. The real reward is with buyers, at least Y &gt; 2X, and it is
less risky to trade.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Strongly bearish combination:</strong> sellers dominate
and can pull the market down, controlled by fear. The real reward is
with sellers, at least Y &gt; 2X, and you can go short safely.</p></li>

@@b1718 p
There are specific degrees between strongly bullish, bullish and even
neutral. Each degree can produce Y &gt; X, Y &lt; X or Y = X. Recording
this is very important for feedback and for building a library.

@@b1719 h3
The Goodman process:
the market is a movie

@@b1720 p
The Goodman process is an ability to connect with the market
emotionally. In Goodman, the market is a movie. The movie shows changes
of passion from extreme sadness to extreme happiness.

@@b1721 p
The Overlay combination is the checking part of the movie. When the
market shows one of the Goodman setups, a Setup is set up to go. The
combination shows the forces from different parts of the movie that say:
yes, this setup can go. Each scene tells us the change in passion, up or
down.

@@b1722 p
In brief, each Overlay is one scene; the combination is the full
movie, from the start to the current price. We aim to find the
combination that fits each market. Every market is unique. There is no
duplicate movie.

@@b1723 h4
Justice versus expectation

@@b1724 p
The Overlay combination aims to do justice to each buyer and seller
according to their ability to give me Y &gt; X. "There is not enough
overlay" (Goodman). This means we still cannot do justice to buyers or
sellers: we cannot say whether they can make the setup go.

@@b1725 p
Every trader wants to expect, or guess, that a trade will be
profitable. Very soon they find themselves in conflict with their own
feelings. A guess is not strong evidence that can give us confidence.
Imagine a court of law judging someone by a guess.

@@b1726 h4
Proactive reading

@@b1727 p
In every scene the market shows us how it works with the Overlay,
from smaller Overlays in the lower time frame to the larger scene in the
higher time frame. Read the Overlay by using Propagation. To do justice
to an Overlay, we must look at how the Overlays are interconnected.

@@b1728 h3
A weak combination:
the daily and the 4-hour

@@b1729 p
Suppose we have a 6&amp;6 in a 4-hour chart and want to trade it
against the daily chart, where we have a Matrix Overlay.

@@b1731 p
Figure: A possible 4-hour 6&amp;6 against a daily Matrix Overlay back
at its origin point without a 3-C.

@@b1732 p
Does this Overlay promise me Y &gt; 2X? Let us do justice to it. On
the daily chart, ask: is there any cancellation, a 3-C? If not, we
cannot assume this point is the end. There are still sellers.

@@b1733 p
So I will not take this 6&amp;6. The setup can fail, because the
market has more energy to break down than to move up. Some will say,
"Double bottom, let's buy!" But bigger investors will dump the market by
increasing their selling force.

@@b1739 p

Can this combination give us a good Line? I do not think so. It shows

@@b1742 p
Figure: The rally from the base was sold and price broke to new lows:

@@b1743 p
Then the market moved to new lows. Why? Very simply, the market did
not have enough Overlay. The bigger investors on the daily chart dumped
every up move; they saw the 4-hour rally as an opportunity to sell more.
This example shows how a bad combination increases the risk.
