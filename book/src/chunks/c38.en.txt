Practice on the 3-C
Principle (2023)

@@b2456 p
In this practice I apply the 3-C principle to a real market, White
Sugar on the weekly chart. I take the count of a 6&amp;6 as a box and
stack copies of it upward, to see where the up Propagation reacts and
where it finally halts.

@@b2457 h3
Price as the Meeting of
Desires

@@b2458 p
At any price level there are many people who want to buy or sell. The
chart shows, as waves, their desires to fulfil their objectives. As in
life, every product has a price that depends on how much demand there is
and how much the supply costs. The price of a product represents the
current situation of BOTH demand and supply.

@@b2459 p
There are times when the activity of demand increases, such as
holidays. There are times when the activity of supply decreases, such as
war. This is always reflected in the price level.

@@b2460 p
I see the 3-C principle as a description of the situation of buying
and selling, together with their activity.

Practice 1: White Sugar

@@b2462 h4
Building the boxes

@@b2464 p
Figure: The base count taken as a box and duplicated upward as levels

In Sugar we have a 6&amp;6 Setup with a nice Jumbo (not shown on this
chart). I take the count of the 6&amp;6 as a box. Then I duplicate the
same box many times, until the upward Propagation halts inside one of
these boxes. This gives the levels C1, C2, C3, C4 and C5.

@@b2467 p
Figure: The 6&amp;6 count taken as a box and stacked upward as the

@@b2468 h4
C1 and C2

@@b2470 p
Figure: The first up Propagation — EP-FPS above C1, the SS below, and
EP-SPS above C2. Real data: AUDUSD, 2-hour, 14 Feb – 4 Mar 2022. (The
original chart was White Sugar weekly, 2018–2023; this one shows the
same structure.)

@@b2471 p
Here the principle of Propagation plays an important role. At C1
price makes the EP of the FPS. Then it reacts and develops the EP of the
SS. Watch again how C1 plays an important role in building the SS, which
ends with the EP of the SPS exactly at the C2 price level.

@@b2473 p
Figure: Propagation from C2 — EP-FPS, then the EP-SS falling back to
the TP of the first FPS, near C1. Real data: AUDUSD, 2-hour, 14 Feb – 4
Mar 2022. (The original chart was White Sugar weekly, 2018–2023; this
one shows the same structure.)

@@b2474 p
At C2 price makes the EP of the FPS (Propagation 1). The EP of the SS
ends at the TP of the FPS, above C1. I apply the 3-C principle as a
projection for the SPS.

@@b2475 h4
C3: Under and time hash

@@b2477 p
Figure: At C3 price moves into a trading range — a time hash. Real
data: AUDUSD, 2-hour, 14 Feb – 4 Mar 2022. (The original chart was White
Sugar weekly, 2018–2023, where the range formed exactly at the Under;
this one shows the same structure.)

@@b2478 p
When price hits C3, exactly at the Under, it moves into a trading
range — a time hash — without any reverse Propagation. It can also be
read as a minor Propagation (the green lines), and we can take a count
from it.

@@b2479 h4
C4 and C5: The buying climax

@@b2481 p
Figure: A breakaway count out of the range, ending with EP-SPS at C4
on high volume. Real data: AUDUSD, 2-hour, 14 Feb – 4 Mar 2022. (The
original chart was White Sugar weekly, 2018–2023, running from C4 to C5;
this one shows the same structure one level lower.)

@@b2482 blockquote
<p>1. After the time hash, price hits C4 with a strong close-up bar.
There is no hesitation, and things move very fast until price is very
near C5.</p>
<p>2. I add a breakaway count and a minor Propagation count to support
the picture.</p>
<p>3. The development ends with the EP of the SPS on high volume, which
agrees with the breakaway count. This is a buying climax.</p>
<p>4. Buyers are unable to break the C5 level. This is the EP of the SPS
of the Propagation, so we expect a large reaction for the EP of the
SS.</p>
<p>5. Watch for the sign of a break down through the C4 level, with a
down bar closing on its low.</p>

Conclusion

@@b2484 p
I conclude that the duplication of counts, as in this practice, stays
valid for a long time — here from 2019 to 2023, and it is still
active.

Is That a Setup? (2023)

@@b2486 p
On a 5-minute chart I saw a nice Goldilocks. But is it a trade
candidate? In this short paper I test that question the strict way, by
placing the pattern inside its 1-2-3 on every time frame before I
decide.

@@b2488 p
Figure: A 5-minute chart of the kind where an attractive Goldilocks
first catches the eye. Real data: NZDUSD, 5-minute, 3–4 March 2022. (The
original chart was GBPJPY M5, 11–15 August 2023; this one shows the same
structure.)

@@b2489 h3
The Rule: A Setup Lives
Inside a 1-2-3

@@b2490 p
Everything in Goodman is based on the 1-2-3. So I must analyze any
Setup strictly within a 1-2-3. The Setup in Goodman reads simply: there
is a 1, and we trade the 2 for the 3.

@@b2492 p
Figure: The Goodman Setup: the 1 is complete, trade the 2, target the
3.

@@b2497 h3
The 4-Hour
Picture: Equilibrium and the Time-hash

@@b2498 p
On the 4-hour (redrawn here on 2-hour data), after the 3-C the SPS
went into equilibrium for several days, from 10 August to 14 August in
my original chart. On 14 August at 08:00 it broke the time-hash into
Line mode. Then it hesitated over the last few bars as inside bars,
forming another time-hash.

@@b2499 p
So many time-hashes made me doubt the short. If there were real panic
in the market, I would expect wide-spread bars that increase the fear. I
did not see them.

@@b2501 p
Figure: The MO line, the SPS and the time hash where price paused
before breaking into Line mode. Real data: AUDUSD, 2-hour, 22 Feb – 4
Mar 2022. (The original chart was GBPJPY H4, 28 July – 15 August 2023;
this one shows the same structure.)

Trading the Line (2021)

@@b2516 p
This paper is about Trading The Line (TTL). I want to show why the
current moment is all a trader really owns, and how to manage a trade
from one moment to the next. Then I walk through a real trade, raising
the stop with every new propagation. I first traded it on EURUSD; the
charts here redraw the same structure on real AUDUSD 1-hour data.
