The Simple System Setup (3S)

@@b3466 p
3S is the shortest Goodman route to a trade: one setup, start to
finish, with entries marked and a target box. It became our everyday
language. On 2024-12-24 Michael wrote: <em>"gbpusd also now nice 3s
15m!!"</em> On 2026-03-09 he wrote: <em>"yes, silver 1d 3s though i not
do count."</em> The text is his. I drew its cover and later built the 3S
day-trading course around it.

@@b3467 p
What it meant: 3S was the setup we actually traded together, so it
was the easiest to teach.

The Racing Bike
(2023-03-10, 2026-03-16)

@@b3494 p
In sixth grade, in 1962, Michael saw a picture of a ten-speed racing
bike in a magazine. "I totally fall in love, want one, want to race
bicycles, be in Tour de France someday... trust me, very unusual for
American boy 1962 - baseball was kid's dream." For two summers he rode
his old Schwinn eight or nine miles across Denver once a week to a shop
that sold racing bikes, just to look at them and talk racing.

@@b3495 p
He got his bike, trained hard and won the Colorado Junior State
Championship. "I tell people bike cost $250, they ready to send me to
nut house." In 1966 a local company sent five riders, Michael among
them, to amateur races in France. "First race I come in 12 of about
20... Second race 13 year old French boy pass me in sprint like I pedal
backwards. End of Tour de France dream, I come home early."

@@b3496 p
He never stopped laughing at himself about it. "Like chess, no talent
but did OK by hard work."

@@b3497 p
<em>The lesson:</em> hard work can make you a state champion; only
the wider arena tells you where you really stand. A trader who wins in a
small pond should expect a faster rider somewhere, and keep his size
modest until he meets him.

@@b3498 h3
"You Like My Car, Son?"
(2024-06-02)

@@b3499 p
When Michael was in fifth grade, a white 1960 Corvette with a red
interior was often parked a block from his school, and he stopped to
look at it every morning. One day a voice behind him said, "You like my
car, son?" It was the owner, a tall man in a business suit. The school
was only a block away, so he drove Michael around for half a mile and
let him out at the front door. "Of course everyone look, see me get out
of nice Corvette!!"

@@b3500 p
About twenty-five years later Michael had a Ferrari, parked behind
his office. His secretary came in to say a kid was looking at the car
and maybe he should shoo him away. Michael went down. The boy was about
twelve. "I say, 'You like my car, son?' He say yes, so I take him for
short ride, then when back let him sit in driver seat for a couple
minutes." His summary: "Pass around good karma!!!"

@@b3501 p
<em>The lesson:</em> someone once gave you a moment you never forgot;
when your turn comes, give it back. Teaching works the same way: what a
mentor gave you freely, pass on freely.
