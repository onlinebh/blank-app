@@b1008 h3
Part 2: Market noise
and the Goodman Box

@@b1009 h3
Is the market predictable?

@@b1010 p
The market can only go up or down, yet every timeframe has its own
trend, which confuses everyone. At every single price there is a
different opinion, a different impression force. To make this clear, let
us look at a few bars, on any timeframe from 1 minute to 1 month.

@@b1012 p
Figure: The same bars read four ways: a strong up bar, shifting
energy, a lie in shifting, and a temporary pause.

@@b1013 h4
One bar

@@b1014 p
The first bar tells me without doubt that the market opened very low
and buyers bid it up to close near the high. Instead of taking that as
true, I ask: is this bar honest enough to promise me the next bar? Is
the energy to move up real?

@@b1015 p
What caused the bar is the energy inside the buyers. The bar itself
is only noise. The question is whether that energy can carry price to
the next level or not. In most cases, a chart cannot describe the real
energy behind each move. The chart is only a tool that shows the noise
of that energy.

@@b1016 h4
Shifting energy, and
the lie in shifting

@@b1017 p
The energy can shift. When it does, we know at least that there was
no real energy in the first up bar. Here we have shifting energy.

@@b1018 p
Even the down move can be false. There is real shifting, but it is
false. I call it a lie in shifting: a fox movement. So where is the
truth? Someone may say the truth comes after the false move, with the
next up bar. Maybe right, but wrong.

@@b1019 p
The effect of the false move remains: it looks like a new low, which
shows that real energy is still there, somewhere at the new low. That
false move also acts as a magnetic field for all sellers who want to
sell. I call this a temporary pause in energy: the real energy is still
unstable. That is why it is so important to qualify a setup. Is there
real energy behind that up bar, or is it just a false move made by
profit-takers?

@@b1020 h4
General versus specific

@@b1021 p
Here I see the general against the specific. The specific is the
close at the high. The general is the downward movement of the larger
trend, outside the picture. The close shows some energy to move up, but
not enough to fight the higher timeframe that wants to push price
down.

@@b1022 p
When those buyers fail, we know that all buyers who could pull the
market up a little after a false breakdown hold a delusional belief.
That up move is noise; it does not show the real energy that lasts. One
picture is not enough. There is also what I call controlled energy,
which no one can deny: the last down bar shows who dominates the
market.

@@b1023 h4
Six cases in the market

@@b1024 p
So is the market predictable? Most of the time, no, until we know the
real energy that lasts and go with it. Much movement is delusional and
cannot describe the energy that will dominate for a long time. I find
six cases:

@@b1025 blockquote
<p>1. The current movement may tell the true energy, or may not.</p>
<p>2. There is a false move and a true move, but even the true move may
be delusional and may or may not describe the true energy of case 1.</p>
<p>3. Energy can shift from buying to selling and back. That shift may
or may not describe the absolute true energy that dominates for a long
time.</p>
<p>4. There is the general against the specific. Sometimes the specific
changes the general, when the general is too weak to fight the current
movement.</p>
<p>5. There is a sign no one can deny that describes the real
energy.</p>
<p>6. Sometimes buyers or sellers are in a delusional area.</p>

@@b1026 p
All six cases apply to every 1-2-3 in the market, and to every
Crosspoint and Spread Triple.

@@b1027 h3
The Goodman Box (G-box)

@@b1028 p
A Goodman Box is a box drawn around successive 1-2-3s, or around the
BP and EP of a Propagation. It helps show the dominant trend, where the
real energy is. We must always redraw the box.

@@b1029 p
We can use the box for <strong>tests</strong> and
<strong>exceeds</strong>: tests happen near the BP, exceeds near the
EP.

@@b1030 ul
<li><p><strong>Test:</strong> a confirmation tool. No one can deny where
the real energy is. A down 1-2-3 inside a small G-box that cannot exceed
it is also a test.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Exceed:</strong> shows the true energy behind the current
move. It can be evidence that price will break the bigger G-box and
propagate into a larger box.</p></li>

@@b1031 p
Zoom out: G-box, then Larger G-box, then Jumbo G-box. Zoom in: Jumbo
G-box, then Larger G-box, then G-box. I recommend giving each a fixed
colour and name.

@@b1032 p
What happens when the market stays a long time inside a box? It is in
energy mode, charging the battery. The G-box can be used as an Overlay;
a Jumbo box at the BP or EP acts as a Mo Overlay, and there you may find
a setup. It is very useful to combine the Spread Triple and the
Crosspoint with the G-box to detect where the real energy is. The G-box
also helps with volume, to measure the response of real energy, and to
measure a jump from one level to the next, as with the Spread
Triple.

@@b1033 p
What if a G-box breaks out falsely at the EP or BP? Then we measure
the general energy to decide. <em>Always redraw the box from smaller to
larger.</em>

@@b1034 h3
A worked example
(first studied on AUDUSD)

@@b1035 p
Suppose today is 10 May on AUDUSD, as in my original chart; here the
same structure is shown on real EURJPY data. We want to know which way
the real force points.

@@b1037 p
Figure: The chart on the day of the decision: do we go long, short,
or stay away? Real data: EURJPY, 30-minute, 24–28 February 2022. (The
original chart was AUDUSD daily, September 2020 to 10 May 2021; this one
shows the same structure.)

@@b1038 p
It looks long. Let us go long? Wrong. We should first find the real
force among all the noise, if we can; many times we cannot.

@@b1039 p
<strong>What do I see now?</strong> I draw the 1-2-3 for a bigger
Matrix, to find which 1-2-3 cycle the market is in. The market has
completed a 1-2-3 cycle. But is the energy in it cancelled or not? That
is not enough, so we look at the origin of the 1-2-3 and do a 3-C.

@@b1041 p
Figure: G-boxes around the legs of the 1-2-3, with the 3-C level near
the current price. Real data: EURJPY, 30-minute, 24–28 February 2022.
(The original chart was AUDUSD daily, September 2020 to 10 May 2021;
this one shows the same structure.)

@@b1042 p
Price is very near the 3-C, so we may get some carryover to the next
1-2-3. But the wisdom I take from the 3-C is that this 1-2-3 is not
strong enough to make an Under, or even an Over. The market sits inside
the G-box and cannot exceed the EP.

@@b1043 p
Now the Crosspoint. If the 1-2-3 holds a lot of energy, its battery
is full. What do I see now? The 1-2-3 is slow: compare the range of the
FPS box with the SPS box. The Crosspoint shows nothing; it sits almost
at 50% of the SS. We will watch it when it propagates its angle into the
next 1-2-3. We work from the most general to the most specific.

@@b1044 p
When the market finishes a cycle, it most often stops at the Return
Zone, the EP-SS. Then we watch whether it can complete a new Propagation
cycle. Here the market breaks down, then reverses to start a new
Propagation 1-2-3. I want to see where the last price lives in each
cycle, and the carryover from the box, so I know which Propagation cycle
we are in. Again, the market cannot take the 3-C carryover.

@@b1045 p
This is the key problem: if we do not know which cycle the current
price is in, we cannot solve the problem. Tell me the problem and I can
find a solution. But with no problem, what do you want, man?

@@b1047 p
Figure: A new Propagation from the Return Point, with its Crosspoint
showing a very slow angle. Real data: EURJPY, 30-minute, 24–28 February
2022. (The original chart was AUDUSD daily, September 2020 to 10 May
2021; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b1048 p
<strong>What do I see now?</strong> Can price exceed the G-box at the
red line? Or will the new Propagation fail to complete its cycle so that
the market moves down? The Crosspoint of the new Propagation shows a
very slow angle: the impression force is weak. That confirms when the EP
of the SS is the end. I see weakness. I accept the fact: the buying
force is weak.

@@b1050 p
Figure: The Spread Triple across three jumps: a good high after jump
1, a bad high after jump 2, and an extremely bad high after jump 3. Real
data: EURJPY, 30-minute, 24–28 February 2022. (The original chart was
AUDUSD daily, September 2020 to 10 May 2021; this one shows the same
structure.)

@@b1051 p
With the Spread Triple I check the last three jumps and their
response. The elastic energy in jumps 1 and 2 decreases over time, and
jump 3 is extremely bad. What do I see now? It is very clear that the
true force is with the sellers whenever the EP-SPS appears. Buyers are
weak. When that happens we can say there is no complete cycle, and it is
a good point to go short.

@@b1052 p
Our next problem is to find whether the current point is the EP of
the SPS. If so, the study suggests selling, with a nested setup.

@@b1054 p
Figure: At the Return Point a Goldilocks setup forms, with an extreme
Crosspoint arrow. Real data: EURJPY, 30-minute, 24–28 February 2022.
(The original chart was AUDUSD daily, September 2020 to 10 May 2021;
this one shows the same structure.)

@@b1055 p
At the same EP-SS of the down 1-2-3, the red arrow with its
Crosspoint shows an extreme impression force. That is good, but most of
the time it signals a climax when it comes with a very small jump. It is
best to do a 3-C to confirm a cancellation. There is so much to do in
Goodman that you should be busy all the time.

@@b1056 p
The market is exactly at the EP-SS of the down 1-2-3, and it shows a
reversal bar. We go short on this bar; it is the same behaviour. When
the market touches the Return Point, it confirms the stop loss at the
high. The market should not touch the red box.

@@b1057 h4
Second chance

@@b1059 p
Figure: A second chance after an incomplete cycle: the Spread Triple
ends in a bad jump, then price falls through the Return Point. Real
data: EURJPY, 30-minute, 25 February – 2 March 2022. (The original chart
was AUDUSD daily, date not shown; this one shows the same
structure.)

@@b1060 p
When the market shows an incomplete cycle after a Propagation, it can
be a second chance. See how well the Spread Triple works: a bad jump at
the end. All the energy shows the market shifting from buying to
selling, an EP-SPS without a complete cycle. Then the market moves down
and down.

@@b1061 p
Can you see how cycles and energy control the market? I did not use
any volume. Do I need it?

@@b1062 h4
A note on volume

@@b1063 p
Still, here are some suggestions on volume. I check the new buyers
who build elastic energy in every jump.

@@b1065 p
Figure: Volume at each numbered point of the cycle, from jump 1 to
the high after jump 3. Real data: EURJPY, 30-minute, 24–28 February
2022. (The original chart was AUDUSD daily, September 2020 to 10 May
2021; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b1066 blockquote
<p>1. Jump 1 shows how energy is stored, with its amount of trading.</p>
<p>2. Jump 2 is the same as jump 1.</p>
<p>3. The danger comes when the EP-SPS holds a lot of trading, as at
number 3: sellers now have more elastic energy. The next jump confirms
this if there is volume.</p>
<p>4. Jump 3, number 4, shows no great energy: the sellers' impression
force is greater than the buyers'.</p>
<p>5. Number 5 shows new sellers' impression force taking control.</p>

@@b1067 p
All the evidence puts the true energy with the sellers. The last SPS
is just a false move, a delusion.

@@b1068 h4
Reading by G-box

@@b1069 p
Instead of reading the 1-2-3, we can read the same story with
G-boxes.

@@b1071 p
Figure: Small G-boxes build a 1-2-3 that becomes a larger box, with
the shaded box as the Return Zone. Real data: USDCAD, 1-hour, 21
February – 1 March 2022. (The original chart was AUDUSD daily, date not
shown; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b1072 p
Here we have a 1-2-3 of G-boxes that can later become a larger box,
and that becomes the Return Zone, the purple box. G-boxes are even more
interesting with time. The longer price stays without a sharp move, the
more it shows that the real energy is to the downside. See how fast the
red move is, while the green move is very slow: there is no energy to
the upside.

@@b1073 p
Where there is no white space, it suggests the energy is for the
opposite direction. Not always, but it is worth noting.

@@b1075 p
Figure: White space inside the G-box stores a good charge of energy,
followed by a strong jump. Real data: AUDUSD, 1-hour, 28 February – 4
March 2022. (The original chart was AUDUSD daily, September 2020 to
February 2021; this one shows the same structure.)

@@b1076 p
I asked myself: if every box charges energy, why do we not always see
a greater jump in the Spread Triple? Look here: the G-box charged its
battery, and it gave me a good jump.

@@b1077 p
This is the end of the story: one of Goodman's most hidden secrets in
the world, and an effective way to trade the market.
